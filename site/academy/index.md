# The Academy

## Start here: you do NOT need to know deck construction

If you are completely new to MECCG, do not begin by shuffling a pile of cards and guessing what belongs where.

The Academy teaches deck construction and table layout before asking you to play Game 1.

For the first solo lessons, use a **two-deck teaching format**:

- **Hero Resource deck**
- **Hazard deck**

This is intentionally smaller than a full tournament construction exercise. The purpose is to learn the game loop first.

The historical Challenge Deck format uses 30 resources and 30 hazards, which is the baseline used by this Academy's first teaching games. The full official construction rules allow a standard play deck to contain 30–50 resources and an equal number of hazards, plus characters and avatars. 

## 1. What are all these piles?

A beginner should physically separate the cards into these areas.

### A. Location deck

This is **not** your normal draw deck.

Put your site cards in a separate face-up/accessible stack. You select the site you need when movement is declared rather than randomly drawing sites.

For the Academy, keep the required sites together in a labelled **LOCATION DECK**.

### B. Hero play deck

This is the main shuffled deck containing:

- Resource cards
- Character cards
- Your avatar, where applicable

For the teaching game, keep the Resource cards together with the starting characters specified by the chosen setup.

### C. Hazard deck

This is the opposing shuffled deck.

For solo play, you become the Hazard player during the Hero company's Movement/Hazard Phase.

### D. Sideboard

Keep this separate and face down.

Do not mix it into the play deck.

The sideboard is an advanced tool for later lessons. The Academy deliberately keeps it out of Game 1 so you can learn the basic game before learning sideboarding.

### E. Character pool

Keep your possible starting/recruitable characters separate until the setup procedure calls for them.

Do not shuffle the whole collection into the Hero deck.

### F. Discard piles

Keep **Hero discard** and **Hazard discard** separate.

Never put discarded Hero resources into the Hazard discard pile, or vice versa.

## 2. What goes into the Hero deck?

For the Academy's first teaching deck, think in layers:

### Characters

These are the people who physically form your company.

Your starting company is selected before play.

### Resources

These are the cards that help the company:

- move;
- avoid attacks;
- survive;
- fight;
- influence;
- acquire objectives;
- develop the company.

The Game 1 Hero resource list is built around these functions rather than simply maximising card power.

### Avatar

A Wizard avatar is used in the normal Wizard format. The avatar is a character card and is part of the player's overall deck construction.

For the first lessons, follow the Academy's specified avatar/setup rather than trying to construct an avatar package yourself.

## 3. What goes into the Hazard deck?

The Hazard deck is not a second Hero deck.

It contains the threats that can interfere with the company.

For Game 1, use:

- Creatures
- Hazard events
- A small number of amplification effects

The Training Hazard deck intentionally teaches the basic sequence:

**LEGALITY → TARGET → DISRUPTION → SEQUENCING → PRESSURE**

You should learn to ask whether a Hazard is useful before playing it.

## 4. The physical table layout

Set the table up like this:

```
                  LOCATION DECK
               [ SITE / REGION CARDS ]

 HERO SIDE                                  HAZARD SIDE

 HERO DECK       HERO COMPANY       HAZARD DECK
 [draw]          [characters]       [draw]
                 [items/resources]

 HERO DISCARD                         HAZARD DISCARD

 SIDEBOARD                             SIDEBOARD
 POOL
```

The exact left/right orientation does not matter. **Keeping the zones physically distinct does.**

For solo play, you are both players. Do not merge the two sides just because you are controlling both.

## 5. Card orientation

Use the normal physical conventions:

- **Untapped:** upright
- **Tapped:** rotate 90°
- **Wounded:** rotate 180°

Keep characters in the company together.

Keep the company visibly separate from your deck and discard pile.

This makes it much easier to see:

- who is available;
- who is tapped;
- who is wounded;
- who controls followers;
- what items are being borne.

## 6. Where should cards go during play?

### In your hand

Keep Hero cards in your hand.

Do not place them face up unless a rule requires it.

### In the company

Characters and their controlled items/resources stay together.

### Stored cards

When a card is stored, move it to a clearly marked **STORED** area or beneath the appropriate site/company area.

### Discard

Discarded Hero cards go into the Hero discard pile.

Discarded Hazards go into the Hazard discard pile.

### Removed from play

Keep removed cards in a separate pile.

Do not accidentally recycle them into the discard pile.

## 7. How to build the first teaching deck

Do **not** start by asking:

> What are the strongest 30 cards?

Start by asking:

> What does my deck need to teach me?

The first Hero deck should contain cards for:

| Function | What it teaches |
|---|---|
| Movement | How to reach useful sites |
| Avoidance | How not to fight everything |
| Survival | How to preserve the company |
| Combat | How to choose fights |
| Objective | How to make progress |
| Compression | How to make one expedition do several jobs |
| Development | How to improve the company |

The first Hazard deck should deliberately mirror those lessons.

## 8. The most important beginner rule

**Do not shuffle everything together.**

A common beginner mistake is to see “deck” as one pile.

MECCG is deliberately built around several different card zones.

Before Game 1 begins, you should be able to point at every pile and say:

> “I know what this pile is, when I draw from it, and whether it gets shuffled.”

If you cannot do that yet, you are not behind. **That is exactly what the Academy is here to teach.**

## 9. Your first physical setup

For Game 1, put on the table:

### Hero

- Starting characters
- Hero Resource deck
- Hero discard pile
- Location deck
- Sideboard, kept separate
- Character pool, kept separate

### Hazard

- Training Hazard deck
- Hazard discard pile
- Hazard sideboard, kept separate

### Shared

- Dice/counters as required
- Region map/reference
- Rule/reference cards if desired

Then stop.

Do not add every expansion.

Do not add Dragons.

Do not add advanced sideboarding.

Do not build a huge collection-based deck yet.

**The goal is to get your first expedition onto the table.**

## 10. Learning sequence

**SET UP → DRAW → ORGANISE → MOVE → EXPOSE → SURVIVE → ENTER → ATTEMPT → RECOVER → REPEAT**

Only after that foundation is comfortable do we expand into:

**DECK CONSTRUCTION → FREE MOVEMENT → ROUTE OPTIMISATION → HAZARD SEQUENCING → CAMPAIGN BUILDING**

## The four questions before every expedition

1. What game am I playing?
2. Where is my first expedition?
3. What does success look like?
4. What makes me turn back?

## Expedition philosophy

Think in expeditions, not turns.

Every expedition has a **Mission, Route, Rewards, Risks and Recovery Plan**.

## Tempo

**Tempo = Progress − Recovery**

## Expedition Triangle

Balance Preparation, Safety and Progress.

## Hero Compass

Consider Safety, Reward, Preparation and Progress.

## Captain's Commandments

1. Never travel without purpose.
2. Never spend a turn you cannot justify.
3. Every expedition must strengthen the company.
4. Untapped characters are opportunities.
5. Protect the Wizard.
6. Recover only when necessary.
7. Always know the next destination.
8. Every site must earn its place.
9. Fear wasted time more than difficult combat.
10. Return stronger than you departed.
11. Plans are commitments, not chains.
12. A plan is a tool, not a prison.

## Learning sequence

**MOVE → AVOID → SURVIVE → FIGHT → ACQUIRE → COMPRESS → DEVELOP → POSITION → CORRUPTION → ABORT → CAMPAIGN**
