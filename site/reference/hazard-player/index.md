# Hazard Player

**In solo play, your Hazard side should make the strongest legal choices available under the selected solo procedure.**

The Academy uses the solo instruction to play as many drawn Hazards as possible within the company's Hazard Limit, choosing the most disadvantageous legal combination. This differs from ordinary two-player discretion, where the Hazard Limit is a ceiling and a human opponent may simply stop.

## Seven-step procedure

### 1 — CAN I PLAY IT?
Check the printed requirements, target, site path, region/site key and timing.

### 2 — WHAT IS THE EXPEDITION?
Identify the company's objective: acquire an item, play a faction, recruit, recover, or reposition.

### 3 — WHO OR WHAT IS CRITICAL?
Identify the character, resource or condition whose disruption would most damage the expedition. Do not assume the character with the lowest prowess is automatically the best target.

### 4 — WHAT IS THE MOST DAMAGING LEGAL PLAY?
Compare the actual consequences of each legal Hazard. A disruption that prevents the objective may be more effective than an attack that looks stronger on paper.

### 5 — WHAT ORDER MAKES SENSE?
Read the exact wording. Check whether a card strengthens another, changes the environment, or must be played before a target is resolved. Never invent a combo from a card's theme alone.

### 6 — WHAT IS LEFT OF THE LIMIT?
Each Hazard card that counts against the limit uses one slot. The base limit is the company's size or two, whichever is greater; **Hobbits count as half a character, rounded up**. It is set at the beginning of the company's Movement/Hazard Phase and stays fixed for that phase, even if a character is later eliminated. Applicable rules and card effects may modify the limit.

### 7 — FILL THE AVAILABLE LEGAL OPPORTUNITY
Under this Academy's selected solo procedure, play as many of the drawn Hazards as possible within the limit. Do not play illegal cards, exceed the limit or ignore printed timing requirements. If several combinations are legal, choose the most disadvantageous one.

## How to tell which Hazards are relevant

Do not decide relevance by theme or by whether a card looks scary. A Hazard is a candidate only if it meets the rules and the card's own requirements in the current game state.

Use this check in order:

1. **Lock in the journey.** Identify the company's current site, declared destination, and route/regions. Do this before revealing Hazards; do not change the route just because you saw a card.
2. **Check the company.** Note the characters, races, skills, prowess, special abilities, equipment, and current condition. These show what is vulnerable and what defences are available; they do not make an otherwise illegal card legal.
3. **Read the whole Hazard.** Check its play conditions, target, keying requirements, region/site restrictions, timing, number of attacks, and any condition that must already be true.
4. **Check the current state.** Verify that the required site, region, character, item, or other condition actually exists now. A card that might be useful later is not necessarily playable now.
5. **Apply the rules and limits.** Check the Hazard Limit and any card-specific restrictions. Do not add extra attacks or waive restrictions to make the game harder.
6. **Choose the strongest legal play.** Among the legal options, use the one that most threatens the company's objective or future survival. If the solo procedure says to play as many drawn Hazards as possible, follow that instruction within the limit.

**Important:** relevant does not mean automatically playable. A creature may be dangerous to this company but still be illegal because the route does not meet its keying requirements, the target is unavailable, or the timing is wrong.

## How to stop yourself going easy on yourself

Use these safeguards every game:

- **No hindsight changes:** once the route and destination are declared, keep them unless a rule or card changes them.
- **No selective draws:** draw randomly from the shuffled Hazard deck. Do not search for a particular creature or redraw because the first cards are inconvenient.
- **No quiet cancellations:** do not ignore an attack, change a die roll, waive corruption, or rescue a character unless a rule or card allows it.
- **Use the best legal option:** when multiple legal targets, keying choices, or combinations are available, choose the most damaging one permitted by the rules.
- **Keep a legality record:** note Hazards you could not play and the exact reason—wrong region, wrong target, timing, missing condition, or limit. This makes it easier to spot accidental leniency.
- **Do not invent extra difficulty:** fair solo play means applying the rules consistently, not creating extra attacks or ignoring restrictions.

## Quick Hazard legality checklist

Before playing a revealed Hazard, ask:

- Does it meet the printed play conditions?
- Does the route or destination allow it?
- Can it legally target this company or character?
- Are timing, attack resolution, and Hazard Limit rules satisfied?
- If it is legal, am I using the most threatening valid option?

If you cannot answer, pause and check the card text and rules rather than automatically ruling in your own favour.

## Example: eight cards, five slots

Moria has site number 3, so the Academy's solo procedure draws eight Hazard cards. A five-character company has a base Hazard Limit of five. You must evaluate all eight cards, but can play no more than five cards that count against the limit. Choose the most damaging legal combination; discard the unplayed cards as required by the chosen solo procedure.

This is an example under the Academy procedure, not a universal rule for every solo variant. Always follow the draw and discard instructions of the scenario you selected.

## Three questions after the phase

- **What did the route expose?** Which site-path region types and names made the creatures legal?
- **What mattered most?** Which Hazard actually threatened the objective or the company's future?
- **What did I learn?** Would a different legal sequence have been more damaging?
