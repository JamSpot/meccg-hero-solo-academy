# Hazard Player

**In solo play, your Hazard side should make the strongest legal choices available under the selected solo procedure.**

The Academy uses the solo instruction to play as many drawn Hazards as possible within the company's Hazard Limit, choosing the most disadvantageous legal combination. This differs from ordinary two-player discretion, where the Hazard Limit is a ceiling and a human opponent may simply stop.

## Seven-step procedure

### 1 — CAN I PLAY IT?
Check the printed requirements, target, site path, region/site key and timing.

### 2 — WHAT IS THE EXPEDITION?
Identify the company's objective: acquire an item, play a faction, recruit, recover, or reposition.

### 3 — WHO OR WHAT IS CRITICAL?
Identify the character, resource or condition whose disruption would most damage the expedition. Do not assume the character with the lowest prowess is automatically the best target.

### 4 — WHAT IS THE MOST DAMAGING LEGAL PLAY?
Compare the actual consequences of each legal Hazard. A disruption that prevents the objective may be more effective than an attack that looks stronger on paper.

### 5 — WHAT ORDER MAKES SENSE?
Read the exact wording. Check whether a card strengthens another, changes the environment, or must be played before a target is resolved. Never invent a combo from a card's theme alone.

### 6 — WHAT IS LEFT OF THE LIMIT?
Each Hazard card that counts against the limit uses one slot. The base limit is the company's size or two, whichever is greater; **Hobbits and Orc scouts each count as half a character, rounded up**. It is set at the beginning of the company's Movement/Hazard Phase and stays fixed for that phase, even if a character is later eliminated. Applicable rules and card effects may modify the limit.

**Do not count Twilight as an ordinary slot.** It may be played at any point during either player's turn and does not count against the Hazard Limit. As a Hazard, it cancels one Environment card in play or declared earlier in the same chain of effects. It still needs a legal Environment target; it is not an extra creature attack.

### 7 — FILL THE AVAILABLE LEGAL OPPORTUNITY
Under this Academy's selected solo procedure, play as many of the drawn Hazards as possible within the limit. Do not play illegal cards, exceed the limit or ignore printed timing requirements. If several combinations are legal, choose the most disadvantageous one.

## Site automatic attacks are separate from Hazard plays

Moria's four Orc strikes at 7 prowess and Goblin-gate's three Orc strikes at 6 prowess are automatic attacks printed on the Hero site cards. They are faced in the Site Phase if the company chooses to enter; they are not Hazard cards and do not consume Hazard Limit slots.

A Hazard card that legally modifies or adds to an automatic attack is still a Hazard play and normally counts against the Hazard Limit. The Hazard side can legally target an automatic attack at the company's new site during the Movement/Hazard Phase even if the Hero player later declines to enter. Check the card's exact target and timing.

The Hero Blue Mountain Dwarf-hold is a Free-hold with a normal Hazard draw of one and no automatic attack. Do not use the different Minion site-card data in this Hero project.

Practise the distinction in the [Site Entry & Automatic Attack Lab]({{ '/scenarios/site-entry-lab/' | relative_url }}), then check the [model answer]({{ '/scenarios/site-entry-lab/answer-key/' | relative_url }}).

## How to tell which Hazards are relevant

Do not decide relevance by theme or by whether a card looks scary. A Hazard is a candidate only if it meets the rules and the card's own requirements in the current game state.

Use this check in order:

1. **Lock in the journey.** Identify the company's current site, declared destination, and route/regions. Do this before revealing Hazards; do not change the route just because you saw a card.
2. **Check the company.** Note the characters, races, skills, prowess, special abilities, equipment, and current condition. These show what is vulnerable and what defences are available; they do not make an otherwise illegal card legal.
3. **Read the whole Hazard.** Check its play conditions, target, keying requirements, region/site restrictions, timing, number of attacks, and any condition that must already be true.
4. **Check the current state.** Verify that the required site, region, character, item, or other condition actually exists now. A card that might be useful later is not necessarily playable now.
5. **Apply the rules and limits.** Check the Hazard Limit and any card-specific restrictions. Do not add extra attacks or waive restrictions to make the game harder.
6. **Choose the strongest legal play.** Among the legal options, use the one that most threatens the company's objective or future survival. If the solo procedure says to play as many drawn Hazards as possible, follow that instruction within the limit.

**Important:** relevant does not mean automatically playable. A creature may be dangerous to this company but still be illegal because the route does not meet its keying requirements, the target is unavailable, or the timing is wrong.

## Doors of Night: save the right cards, not every awkward draw

The printed *The Wizards* solitaire rules say that Hazard cards requiring Doors of Night are saved and played when a Doors of Night is drawn. If a future Hazard package contains one, keep it in a clearly marked saved area; do not discard it with ordinary unplayed draws. When it becomes available, it still has to meet its own text and fit within the current company's Hazard Limit. The active Academy 30-card list contains no Doors of Night and no card that requires it. Wake of War's extra Wolf bonus is conditional, not a requirement, so Wake of War remains playable without Doors of Night. Ordinary drawn-but-unplayed cards follow the Academy's separate end-of-phase discard convention. Practise this in the [Solo Deck Cycle Lab]({{ '/scenarios/solo-deck-cycle-lab/' | relative_url }}).


## How to stop yourself going easy on yourself

Use these safeguards every game:

- **No hindsight changes:** once the route and destination are declared, keep them unless a rule or card changes them.
- **No selective draws:** draw randomly from the shuffled Hazard deck. Do not search for a particular creature or redraw because the first cards are inconvenient.
- **No quiet cancellations:** do not ignore an attack, change a die roll, waive corruption, or rescue a character unless a rule or card allows it.
- **Use the best legal option:** when multiple legal targets, keying choices, or combinations are available, choose the most damaging one permitted by the rules.
- **Keep a legality record:** note Hazards you could not play and the exact reason—wrong region, wrong target, timing, missing condition, or limit. This makes it easier to spot accidental leniency.
- **Do not invent extra difficulty:** fair solo play means applying the rules consistently, not creating extra attacks or ignoring restrictions.

## Quick Hazard legality checklist

Before playing a revealed Hazard, ask:

- Does it meet the printed play conditions?
- Does the route or destination allow it?
- Can it legally target this company or character?
- Are timing, attack resolution, and Hazard Limit rules satisfied?
- If it is legal, am I using the most threatening valid option?

If you cannot answer, pause and check the card text and rules rather than automatically ruling in your own favour. Then practise with the [Hazard Legality Workshop]({{ '/scenarios/hazard-legality-workshop/' | relative_url }}) and its [model answer]({{ '/scenarios/hazard-legality-workshop/answer-key/' | relative_url }}).

## Example: eight cards, five slots

Moria's normal Hazard draw number is 3, so the Academy's solo procedure draws eight Hazard cards. A five-character company has a base Hazard Limit of five. You must evaluate all eight cards, but can play no more than five ordinary cards that count against the limit. If Twilight is among the draw and has a legal Environment target, it may also be played without using one of those five slots. Choose the most damaging legal combination; discard the unplayed cards as required by the chosen solo procedure.

Before resolving a draw, practise which site's printed Hazard number applies and how it differs from the Hazard Limit in the [Hazard Draw & Limit Lab]({{ '/scenarios/hazard-draw-limit-lab/' | relative_url }}). For a card-by-card distinction between region keys and site keys, complete the [Site-Keying Workshop]({{ '/scenarios/site-keying-workshop/' | relative_url }}) and check its [model answer]({{ '/scenarios/site-keying-workshop/answer-key/' | relative_url }}).

This is an example under the Academy procedure, not a universal rule for every solo variant. Always follow the draw and discard instructions of the scenario you selected.

## Three questions after the phase

- **What did the route expose?** Which site-path region types and names made the creatures legal?
- **What mattered most?** Which Hazard actually threatened the objective or the company's future?
- **What did I learn?** Would a different legal sequence have been more damaging?

References: [Council of Elrond Official Game Rules](https://www.councilofelrond.org/rules/) and the [Academy solo ruleset]({{ '/reference/solo-ruleset/' | relative_url }}).

## River denial: count available Rangers before choosing Seized by Terror

When the route includes a Shadow-land or Dark-domain, Seized by Terror may split an eligible non-Wizard character. If River is also played on the destination site, count the number of Rivers and the number of Rangers who can still tap at the beginning of the Site Phase. One Ranger is required for each River. Target a Ranger only when removing him would leave fewer available Rangers than Rivers; otherwise choose the highest-value legal target by split probability. On the five-Dwarf Iron Hill example, Óin is the only Ranger and splits on 26/36 (72.2%) of rolls; Dori is the higher-probability attrition target at 33/36 (91.7%). See the [Iron Hill River & Seized by Terror Lab]({{ '/scenarios/iron-hill-river-lab/' | relative_url }}) for the full decision table.


## After Seized by Terror succeeds: follow the split through

The current card text excludes both Wizard and Ringwraith characters. If the roll succeeds, the target character forms a different company and that company immediately returns to the original company's site of origin. Do not treat this as an elimination: the target remains in play, and cards borne by it remain with it.

Then check the company structure. The card names the target character, not its followers. A controller separated from its followers breaks their direct-influence relationship; a follower split away from its controller is also no longer co-located with that controller. The affected followers are not immediately charged to general influence, but must be reassigned under general or eligible direct influence by the end of the next Organization Phase or be discarded. Check Fellowship separately: its own text discards it whenever a character or ally leaves the company, so a successful split discards any Fellowship copy in play on that company.

The split-off company returns to the origin, not the declared destination. Under the return-to-origin rule it is not considered to have moved to the new site and cannot initiate actions during its Site Phase. The original company's base Hazard Limit was fixed at the start of its Movement/Hazard Phase; do not recalculate it after the split. The remaining company continues its declared journey unless another effect returns it. The two companies cannot rejoin until they are at the same site. Work through the full consequences in the [Seized by Terror Aftermath Lab]({{ '/scenarios/seized-terror-aftermath/' | relative_url }}) and its [model answer]({{ '/scenarios/seized-terror-aftermath/answer-key/' | relative_url }}).


## Promptings of Wisdom: check before choosing a Seized target

The active Hero deck contains Promptings of Wisdom. CoE Rulings Digest #210, Question 6, is explicit: if Seized by Terror targets the Ranger who bears Promptings, the split still occurs but Promptings follows that Ranger and can cancel the return-to-origin effect if the Ranger taps and the effect resolves first. The Ranger then continues to the new site without a separate Movement/Hazard Phase and rejoins before the Site Phase. The original company loses Promptings' protection for the rest of the turn.

If Seized targets a different character, that character splits and returns to the origin; Promptings remains with the Ranger's original company and does not cancel the target's return. This is a target-selection decision, not a blanket immunity. Promptings also does not cancel River. If the Ranger taps to use Promptings, he makes a corruption check and is no longer available to tap for River that Site Phase. Fellowship still discards when the split character leaves the company, even if it rejoins later.

Read the full [Seized by Terror Aftermath Lab]({{ '/scenarios/seized-terror-aftermath/' | relative_url }}) and its [model answer]({{ '/scenarios/seized-terror-aftermath/answer-key/' | relative_url }}) before treating a successful Seized play as an automatic return to origin.
