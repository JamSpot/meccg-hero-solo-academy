# Hazard Player

**In solo play, your Hazard side should make the strongest legal choices available under the selected solo procedure.**

The Academy uses the solo instruction to play as many drawn Hazards as possible within the company's Hazard Limit, choosing the most disadvantageous legal combination. This differs from ordinary two-player discretion, where the Hazard Limit is a ceiling and a human opponent may simply stop.

## Seven-step procedure

### 1 — CAN I PLAY IT?
Check the printed requirements, target, site path, region/site key and timing.

### 2 — WHAT IS THE EXPEDITION?
Identify the company's objective: acquire an item, play a faction, recruit, recover, or reposition.

### 3 — WHO OR WHAT IS CRITICAL?
Identify the character, resource or condition whose disruption would most damage the expedition. Do not assume the character with the lowest prowess is automatically the best target.

### 4 — WHAT IS THE MOST DAMAGING LEGAL PLAY?
Compare the actual consequences of each legal Hazard. A disruption that prevents the objective may be more effective than an attack that looks stronger on paper.

### 5 — WHAT ORDER MAKES SENSE?
Read the exact wording. Check whether a card strengthens another, changes the environment, or must be played before a target is resolved. Never invent a combo from a card's theme alone.

### 6 — WHAT IS LEFT OF THE LIMIT?
Each Hazard card that counts against the limit uses one slot. The normal base limit is set at the beginning of the company's Movement/Hazard Phase and stays fixed for that phase.

### 7 — FILL THE AVAILABLE LEGAL OPPORTUNITY
Under this Academy's selected solo procedure, play as many of the drawn Hazards as possible within the limit. Do not play illegal cards, exceed the limit or ignore printed timing requirements. If several combinations are legal, choose the most disadvantageous one.

## Example: eight cards, five slots

Moria has site number 3, so the Academy's solo procedure draws eight Hazard cards. A five-character company has a base Hazard Limit of five. You must evaluate all eight cards, but can play no more than five cards that count against the limit. Choose the most damaging legal combination; discard the unplayed cards as required by the chosen solo procedure.

## Three questions after the phase

- **What did the route expose?** Which site-path region types and names made the creatures legal?
- **What mattered most?** Which Hazard actually threatened the objective or the company's future?
- **What did I learn?** Would a different legal sequence have been more damaging?
