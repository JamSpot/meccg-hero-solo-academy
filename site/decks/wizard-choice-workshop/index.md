# Wizard Choice Workshop — Which Wizard?

This is a deck-building review, not a change to the active list. The Academy's current working baseline remains **one Gandalf in a 36-card Hero play deck**. The evidence below shows why that choice is not automatic for a five-card solo game.

## 1. The important deck-construction rule

The current **Council of Elrond consolidated rules (section 1.5)** allow up to **10 non-avatar character cards** and up to **three avatar cards**, with any combination permitted except three different avatars. For a Wizard player, this allows up to three Wizard-avatar cards, including three copies of the same Wizard. Only one avatar can be in play at a time; if it is eliminated, you cannot reveal another avatar. The original printed *The Wizards* rulebook says up to two Wizard cards; the Academy follows the current CoE consolidated rule for current play.

That means these are all distinct questions:

- **36 cards:** 30 Resources + five non-Wizard characters + one Gandalf. This is the current working baseline.
- **37 cards:** 30 Resources + five non-Wizard characters + two copies of Gandalf. This is legal.
- **38 cards:** 30 Resources + five non-Wizard characters + three copies of Gandalf. This is also legal.
- **37 cards:** the same Resource and non-Wizard cards, but two copies of Pallando. This is legal.
- **38 cards:** the historical Challenge Deck C player aid lists 30 Resources, five non-Wizard characters and **Pallando ×3**. This is legal under the current avatar-card limit; it is not a rules discrepancy.

The 36-card Gandalf build is still the active teaching baseline. The original 38-card Pallando list is a valid historical build, but it is not the Wizard choice currently being tested.

The solo procedure requires a separate Hazard deck and a Hero play deck of at least 30 cards. It does not require the Hero play deck to contain exactly 38 cards. The current 36-card build is therefore a valid starting size; the second Wizard is an optional consistency choice, not a missing character slot.

## 2. Card-by-card comparison

| Feature | Gandalf | Pallando |
|---|---|---|
| Skills | Warrior / Scout / Sage / Diplomat | Warrior / Ranger / Sage / Diplomat |
| Direct influence | 10 | 10 |
| Prowess / Body | 6 / 9 | 6 / 9 |
| Home site | Any Haven | Grey Havens |
| Distinctive ability | +1 to all his corruption checks; may tap to test a Gold Ring | Controller may keep one more card than normal; opponent discards cards face-up |
| Value in this solo build | Better protection for the Book bearer | Better routine card flow |
| Ability with no target in the current Resources | Gold-ring testing is unused because the list has no Gold Ring | Face-up opponent discard has no opponent hand to affect |

Both Wizards are Sages, so either can use Book of Mazarbûl's Sage-only tap ability to increase hand size by one until the next untap phase. Both have the same direct influence, prowess and body. Their home-site difference does not prevent either from being played at Rivendell under the Wizard-placement rule.

## 3. The other three Wizards — why they are not the current baseline

The Academy should consider all five Wizard cards, not just the two found in the historical Challenge Deck C comparison.

| Wizard | Relevant printed ability | Fit with this Hero/Hazard experiment |
|---|---|---|
| **Alatar** | Reduces by one the number of cards the opponent draws based on his company's movement. He can also join another company from a Haven to face one strike from a Hazard creature. | Very powerful defensively. The solo rule replaces the normal Hazard draw with a formula, so the exact interaction between Alatar's -1 and the solo draw formula needs an explicit ruling before use. Until resolved, he is a poor choice for a test intended to measure the full Hazard pressure. |
| **Gandalf** | +1 to all his corruption checks; can tap to test a Gold Ring. | Strong fit for the Book mission. The Gold Ring ability has no target in the current Resource list, but the corruption bonus directly helps with Book storage. |
| **Pallando** | His controller may keep one more card than normal; opponent discards cards face-up. | Strong fit for a five-card solo hand. The face-up discard instruction has no opponent hand to affect in this format. |
| **Radagast** | +1 to all corruption checks; may draw one additional card for each Wilderness region in his company's site path when moving to a new site. | Potentially the strongest card-flow option on this campaign: the opening routes contain three or four Wilderness regions. He is not a Sage, so he cannot use Book of Mazarbûl's Sage-only hand-size tap ability. The extra Resource draws may also make the Hero side substantially more forgiving. |
| **Saruman** | May tap to use a Palantír; at the beginning of his end-of-turn phase he may tap to retrieve a spell card from the discard pile. | Low synergy with the current active Resource list, which has no Palantír or dedicated spell package. Reconsider only if the deck is rebuilt around those cards. |

### Why the Academy keeps Gandalf for the baseline

Gandalf is not the only powerful choice. He is the most controlled starting point for this particular experiment:

- He protects the Book bearer with +1 to corruption checks.
- He is a Sage, so he can use the Book's own hand-size ability.
- He does not reduce the Hazard draw, unlike Alatar.
- He does not add the large route-dependent Resource draw boost that Radagast would provide.
- His Gold Ring ability is unused, but that does not change the core Book mission.

Pallando remains the most interesting fair alternative: he improves the five-card hand without directly reducing Hazard pressure. Radagast should be a later high-card-flow experiment, not mixed into the first baseline. Alatar should wait until the solo draw interaction is resolved.

## 4. What the choice means in a five-card solo game

### Gandalf: safer Book storage

Book of Mazarbûl has 2 corruption points. Gandalf receives +1 on all his corruption checks. If he bears only the Book, his minimum 2D6 result of 2 becomes 3 after the modifier, which is greater than 2. In that clean case the storage check is guaranteed to pass without tapping another character for support.

This is a real advantage for the first Book mission. It is not an unconditional guarantee if Gandalf has additional corruption points from other cards or effects.

### Pallando: better card flow

Pallando's hand-size ability is always relevant to a solo game that normally uses five cards. It raises the normal hand target by one while he is in play. Since Pallando is also a Sage, he can use the Book's own hand-size ability when he bears it. The face-up discard ability has no useful target in this solo format, but the extra card does.

For a deck containing several situational avoidance and combat Resources, seeing one more card can make it easier to hold the right answer while still finding the Book, equipment or next character.

### The practical trade-off

- Choose **Gandalf** when the teaching priority is making the Book's corruption check as safe and simple as possible.
- Choose **Pallando** when the teaching priority is card selection and maintaining a larger hand throughout the expedition.
- Do not count Pallando's face-up discard instruction as an advantage against the automated Hazard deck.
- Do not count Gandalf's Gold Ring test as a benefit in the current list; there is no Gold Ring Resource to test.

The five starting Dwarves can support a Pallando Book bearer by tapping a character for +1 to a corruption check. That means Gandalf's corruption bonus is useful, but it is not the only way to make the Book storage check safe.

## 5. Opening-hand consistency: one Wizard or two?

The solitaire opening hand is five cards. If the Hero play deck is shuffled uniformly:

| Build | Deck size | Wizard copies | Chance to see at least one in opening five |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Current Gandalf baseline | 36 | 1 | 13.9% |
| Two-Gandalf variant | 37 | 2 | 25.5% |
| Two-Pallando variant | 37 | 2 | 25.5% |
| Three-Gandalf variant | 38 | 3 | 35.3% |
| Historical three-Pallando list | 38 | 3 | 35.3% |

These are opening-hand probabilities, not recommendations. Three copies are legal, but duplicate avatars cannot be played while one is already in play and do not let you replace an eliminated avatar.

A second or third copy increases the chance of seeing the Wizard in the opening five. It does not allow multiple Wizards in play. If duplicates are drawn while the Wizard is already in play, they are redundant; if the Wizard is eliminated, the solo rules do not let you reveal another avatar, so extra copies are not replacement Wizards.

The independent 2017 [My Solo Rules](https://meccg.wordpress.com/my-solo-rules/) author deliberately uses a single Wizard in solo games to make them more challenging. That is a design recommendation, not a mandatory rule. Its three-Wizard statement aligns with current CoE section 1.5; the original printed *The Wizards* two-Wizard limit is the older text.

## 6. Academy recommendation for controlled testing

Do not change the active list mid-test.

1. Keep the **36-card one-Gandalf build** as the current baseline for the first three recorded games.
2. Record whether Gandalf's corruption bonus actually changes a Book storage decision, and whether the opening hand delays playing him.
3. If the deck regularly struggles to find Gandalf, test a **37-card two-Gandalf** version, then (separately) a **38-card three-Gandalf** version.
4. If the deck regularly struggles with hand quality or card flow, test **two or three Pallando** as a separate alternative. The original three-Pallando Challenge Deck C build is a valid 38-card reference. Record the extra card each turn and whether the Book's own hand-size ability is used.
5. Compare one variable at a time over the same route and Hazard baseline. Do not change the Hazard deck at the same time.

The Academy has not silently switched the active Wizard. Gandalf remains the working first-play choice until a controlled comparison justifies changing it.
