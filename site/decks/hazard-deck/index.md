# Hazard Deck Construction — Beginner Guide

## The exact teaching Hazard list

For Game 1 and the first Academy lessons, use the 30-card Hazard portion of the historical **Dwarven Quest / Pallando Challenge Deck C**. This is a teaching baseline, not a claim that it is the strongest possible Hazard deck.

These are genuine physical cards. No proxies are intended.

| Quantity | Card |
|---:|---|
| 3 | Cave Worm |
| 3 | Despair of the Heart |
| 3 | Full of Froth and Rage |
| 3 | Giant Spiders |
| 3 | Lesser Spiders |
| 2 | Neeker-breekers |
| 2 | River |
| 2 | Seized by Terror |
| 2 | Twilight |
| 2 | Wake of War |
| 2 | Wargs |
| 3 | Watcher in the Water |
| **30** | **Total** |

Keep this Hazard deck separate from the 38-card Hero play deck, Hero discard pile, Location deck and sideboard.

## What the Hazard deck teaches

The Hazard deck is the opponent's toolbox. In solo play, you control it during the Hero company's Movement/Hazard Phase. Your task is to choose the most effective **legal** pressure, not to invent extra rules or target something merely because it looks dramatic.

The Challenge Deck C mix gives repeated practice with:

- **Creature legality and keying:** Cave Worm, Giant Spiders, Lesser Spiders, Neeker-breekers, Wargs and Watcher in the Water.
- **Attack amplification:** Full of Froth and Rage and Wake of War, subject to their exact card text and timing.
- **Disruption and roadblocks:** Despair of the Heart, River and Seized by Terror.
- **Environment interaction:** Twilight.
- **Sequencing:** deciding which legal card should be played first and what should follow.

This is a teaching structure, not a substitute for reading the printed wording of an individual card.

## What the 30 cards actually do

The list is a mixture of creatures, ongoing attack support and disruption. Read the printed card before every play; this table is a quick guide to what to check, not permission to skip the text.

### Route audit — Moria and Goblin-gate are not the same test

| Journey | Normal Hazard draw | Solo draw | Base Hazard Limit | Site automatic attack |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Rivendell → Moria | 3 | **8** | **5** | Orcs — four strikes with 7 prowess |
| Moria → Goblin-gate | 2 | **6** | **5** | Orcs — three strikes with 6 prowess if the company enters |
| Goblin-gate → Blue Mountain Dwarf-hold | 1 | **4** | **5** | None |

The solo draw is **2 + twice the normal Hazard draw**. The Hazard Limit remains separate. The site automatic attack is also separate from the Hazard plays.

On **Rivendell → Moria** (Rhudaur → Hollin → Redhorn Gate), all three regions are Wilderness and Redhorn Gate is named. That makes Cave Worm, Giant Spiders, Lesser Spiders, Wargs, Neeker-breekers and Watcher in the Water candidates under their respective printed keys; Despair of the Heart and River have their own target rules. Seized by Terror is **not** legal on this route because there is no Shadow-land or Dark-domain region.

On **Moria → Goblin-gate** (Redhorn Gate → Hollin → Rhudaur → High Pass), all four regions are Wilderness. The route still makes the Wilderness-keyed creatures candidates and includes Redhorn Gate and High Pass for Cave Worm. Watcher in the Water can use its double-Wilderness key; its special permission to be played at Moria does not apply because Goblin-gate is the new site. Seized by Terror is still illegal: Goblin-gate's site type is Shadow-hold [{S}], not a Shadow-land region [{s}].

On **Goblin-gate → Blue Mountain Dwarf-hold** (High Pass → Rhudaur → Arthedain → Númeriador), all four regions are also Wilderness. The Wilderness-keyed creatures remain candidates, and Cave Worm can be keyed to High Pass. The normal Hazard draw for Blue Mountain Dwarf-hold is one, so the solo draw is four. The site has no automatic attack. The company may choose not to enter Goblin-gate on the previous turn and use it as a waypoint; if it does, it still faces the six-card Hazard draw on arrival, but does not face Goblin-gate's automatic attack or take any site-phase actions.

This is the practical test of fair solo play: the Hazard side should exploit every legal key and target, but must not treat a Shadow-hold site as a Shadow-land region or play a card whose condition is absent.

## Three numbers and rules that must not be confused

### 1. Solo Hazard draw

The Academy uses the solo procedure described on the [Solo Rules page]({{ '/reference/solo-ruleset/' | relative_url }}): draw **2 + twice the normal Hazard draw number for the destination site**.

For the Game 1 move to Moria, the normal Hazard draw number is 3, so the solo draw is:

**2 + (2 × 3) = 8 Hazard cards.**

This tells you how many cards are available to the solo Hazard player for that company's Movement/Hazard Phase. First identify the site whose normal draw number applies under the movement rules; for a move to a Haven, normal card draws are based on the site of origin.

### 2. Base Hazard Limit

The normal base Hazard Limit is the company's size or two, whichever is greater. **Each Hobbit or Orc scout counts as half a character, with the total rounded up; the minimum limit is two.** It is set for that company's Movement/Hazard Phase and remains fixed for that phase, even if the company later loses a character. Other applicable rules or card effects can modify it; check the chosen scenario before using a special case.

For the five-character starting company in Game 1, the base Hazard Limit is **5**. If you later change the company to include Hobbits or Orc scouts, recalculate the limit using the half-character rule instead of simply counting character cards.

### 3. The solo procedure for playing the drawn Hazards

The solo procedure directs you to play **as many of the drawn Hazards as you can within the Hazard Limit**, choosing the combination that is most disadvantageous for your Hero company. Eight cards drawn does not mean eight ordinary limit-counting plays: with a base limit of five, no more than five Hazard cards that count against that limit can be played in the phase.

**Important exception — Twilight:** the Challenge Deck C list contains two copies. Twilight may be played at any point during either player's turn and **does not count against the Hazard Limit**. As a Hazard, it cancels and discards one Environment card in play or declared earlier in the same chain of effects. It is still a Hazard otherwise, and it may also be played as a Resource. So a legal Twilight does not use one of the company's five ordinary Hazard slots. Check its target and timing; it is not a free creature attack.

Do not automatically play the first five cards. Check legality and sequence, then choose the strongest legal combination. Do not deliberately leave a useful legal slot empty merely because a card seems less dramatic. If you are pausing for a teaching discussion, pause before committing the play and explain the competing choices.

## The seven-step Hazard decision

1. **Legality:** can the card be played against this company, its site path, destination site or another legal target?
2. **Key:** if it is a creature, which region type, region name, site type or site name is being used to key it?
3. **Objective:** what is the company trying to achieve this expedition?
4. **Critical character:** whose disruption would most damage that objective?
5. **Sequence:** does the wording of another card make this play stronger before or after it?
6. **Limit:** how many Hazard cards that count against the limit have already been declared?
7. **Best combination:** which legal set of plays creates the greatest disadvantage within the remaining limit?

For double-region keying, check that the company site path contains the required number of matching regions; matching regions need not be consecutive. Check the actual card text and current official rulings before resolving an unusual case.

## Route-first Hazard hand audit

Before selecting a combination, put the declared site path beside the drawn Hazard hand. Sort candidates into these groups, then check the printed wording:

| Group | First check | Decision to make |
|---|---|---|
| **Named-region / site threats** | Is the required region name or destination site actually part of this company's situation? | Keep only if the printed key and every other condition are satisfied. |
| **Region-type creatures** | Does the declared site path contain the required region type(s), including the required count for double/triple keys? | Count the actual path; do not count a region merely because it appears on a map nearby. |
| **Events and support** | Is the target, timing window, condition and duration valid now? | Determine whether it disrupts the mission or improves a legal attack, rather than assuming it combines with every creature. |

For the Academy's Rivendell → Moria example, the Region Movement path Rhudaur → Hollin → Redhorn Gate contains three Wilderness regions and names Redhorn Gate; Moria is the destination site. That makes several cards in the teaching list worth checking, but it does not make every card automatically legal. The [Route Risk Workshop]({{ '/scenarios/route-risk-workshop/' | relative_url }}) gives you a repeatable way to compare routes and record what the actual Hazard hand permits. The [Hero Resource Timing Workshop]({{ '/scenarios/hero-resource-practice/' | relative_url }}) also teaches how the starting Dwarves' skills affect the response to River, Concealment and the Moria attack.

After legality, compare impact and sequence. Count each play against the Hazard Limit according to the normal rules and the card's exact wording, and respect any explicit exception. If you cannot prove an interaction from the physical text or a current ruling, mark it **unverified** and do not rely on it for the play.

## Worked example — Moria

The Hero company of five characters moves to Moria.

- Solo Hazard draw: **8 cards**.
- Base Hazard Limit: **5**.
- Available plays: only cards that are legal and meet their printed requirements.
- Selection: choose the most disadvantageous legal combination, respecting sequencing and the five-card limit.

A card that cannot be legally keyed is not an option, however powerful it looks. A legal event may be more damaging to the expedition than a creature, depending on the company's current state and the card wording. Do not decide by attack prowess alone.

## Route-keying audit — why Moria is a serious test

On the teaching route **Rivendell → Moria** using **Rhudaur → Hollin → Redhorn Gate**, the route and destination make much of the Challenge Deck C creature suite worth checking. This is a legality audit, not a promise that every card will be in the Hazard hand or can all be played in one phase.

| Creature | Why it may be legal on this expedition | Check before playing |
|---|---|---|
| **Cave Worm** | Its printed named-region key includes **Redhorn Gate**, which is in the declared site path. | Confirm the named-region key and all printed conditions. |
| **Giant Spiders** | It can be keyed to **double Wilderness**; this route contains three Wilderness regions. | Confirm the actual site path and the creature's exact key. |
| **Lesser Spiders** | Its keys include Wilderness and Shadow-land region types, plus a Ruins & Lairs [{R}] site key. The route exposes Wilderness. | Moria is a Shadow-hold [{S}], not a Ruins & Lairs site; this route makes the card a candidate via Wilderness, not via Moria's site type. |
| **Wargs** | Its key includes Wilderness. | Check the Wolf attack text and whether Wake of War is active. |
| **Neeker-breekers** | Its keys include Wilderness among its region-type keys; the route exposes Wilderness. | Moria is a Shadow-hold [{S}], not a Ruins & Lairs [{R}] site. The card is a candidate via Wilderness, not via Moria's site type. Read its one-strike-per-character wording exactly. |
| **Watcher in the Water** | Its card text permits play at **Moria**; it also has a double-Wilderness key. | Check its special attack wording and the ruling on attacks where each character faces one strike. |

The lesson is important: **route choice is also Hazard-deck choice**. Rhudaur → Hollin → Redhorn Gate exposes three Wilderness regions and names Redhorn Gate; arriving at Moria adds the explicit Moria option for Watcher in the Water. Moria itself is a Shadow-hold [{S}], not a Ruins & Lairs [{R}] site. The Hazard player must still obey the actual card text, timing, target restrictions and Hazard Limit.

Practise this distinction with the [Site-Keying Workshop]({{ '/scenarios/site-keying-workshop/' | relative_url }}) before attempting the full [Hazard Legality Workshop]({{ '/scenarios/hazard-legality-workshop/' | relative_url }}). See the [official rules]({{ '/reference/solo-ruleset/' | relative_url }}) and [Challenge Deck C player aid](https://meccg.es/meta/cards/list/MECCG-CD-playeraid_v1.0.pdf).

## Physical construction

1. Find the 30 listed Hazard cards and check each quantity.
2. Shuffle them thoroughly.
3. Place the Hazard deck on the opposite side of the table from the Hero deck.
4. Mark a separate Hazard discard pile.
5. Keep any Hazard sideboard cards separate.
6. Do not reveal or pre-arrange the draw order.
7. During each solo Movement/Hazard Phase, draw according to the selected solo procedure, then apply the normal Hazard Limit and card legality.

## After each Hazard phase: discard and recycle correctly

- Hazard cards drawn but not played are discarded at the end of that Movement/Hazard Phase.
- When the Hazard deck is exhausted and more cards must be drawn, shuffle the Hazard discard pile to form a new Hazard deck.
- In this Book of Mazarbul mission, non-unique defeated creatures go to the Hazard discard so they can return when the deck is recycled. Follow the normal rules for unique creatures; this mission does not award kill MPs as its victory objective.
- Keep the Hero play-deck discard and Hazard discard separate. Never put unplayed drawn Hazards back into the deck immediately unless the selected scenario explicitly tells you to.

## Solo play: a fair test, not an easy test

The learner controls both sides, so use a consistent rule: the Hazard side should make the strongest legal choices available under the selected solo procedure. Do not give the Hero side hidden assistance or use knowledge that is not available from the rules and revealed cards. If a decision is unclear, stop and check the card text or rules before resolving it.

## Later Hazard construction

Once Games 1–3 are comfortable, the Academy can move beyond the historical teaching list and build a larger Hazard strategy around creature frequency, amplification, route punishment, site punishment, corruption pressure and campaign disruption. Dragons remain optional advanced material; the first deck is not dragon-heavy.
