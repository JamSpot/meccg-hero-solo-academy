# Hazard Deck Construction — Beginner Guide

## The exact teaching Hazard list

For Game 1 and the first Academy lessons, use the 30-card Hazard portion of the historical **Dwarven Quest / Pallando Challenge Deck C**, adapted for the Gandalf-led Academy. This is a teaching baseline, not a claim that it is the strongest possible Hazard deck.

## Starting balance and what changes during scoring play

The fixed list contains **16 creatures (53%)** and **14 non-creature Hazards (47%)**. That is close to the approximate 50/50 creature/event balance suggested by the independent solo-deck guide.

- **Creatures (16):** Cave Worm ×3, Giant Spiders ×3, Lesser Spiders ×3, Neeker-breekers ×2, Wargs ×2, Watcher in the Water ×3.
- **Non-creatures (14):** Despair of the Heart ×3, Full of Froth and Rage ×3, River ×2, Seized by Terror ×2, Twilight ×2, Wake of War ×2.
- **No Dragon creatures:** Cave Worm is a Drake. This gives the deck a small Drake package without making the baseline Dragon-heavy.
- **Support overlap:** Full of Froth and Rage boosts Spider and Animal attacks; Wake of War boosts Wolf, Spider and Animal attacks. Cave Worm is a Drake and receives neither bonus. Both support events cannot be duplicated; Full of Froth is discarded if a Spider or Animal attack is defeated.
- **Corruption pressure:** The three copies of Despair of the Heart can be spread across three different eligible Dwarves in one Movement/Hazard Phase, subject to the Hazard Limit. The restriction is one corruption card per character per turn, not one per company; Despair also cannot be duplicated on a given character. Each copy must begin a new chain of effects. A wound to any member of the company triggers a check for each Despair target. Dori, Kíli and Óin each have -1 to all their corruption checks, so they are particularly dangerous targets. If the Despair target is also carrying Book of Mazarbul, the Book's 2 corruption points combine with Despair's 2. This is a real baseline combination, not a proposed card swap. Practise it in [Drill 15 — Three Despairs and one wound]({{ '/scenarios/hazard-combo-lab/' | relative_url }}) and check the [model answer]({{ '/scenarios/hazard-combo-lab/answer-key/' | relative_url }}).

In the Book tutorial, the Academy's mission procedure returns defeated non-unique creatures to the Hazard discard. In the full scoring game, defeated creatures go to the Hero MP pile instead. The Hazard deck therefore becomes more event-heavy as creatures are defeated and removed from future cycles. See the [Full Solo Scoring Trial]({{ '/scenarios/full-solo-scoring-trial/' | relative_url }}) before changing between those two modes.

**Hero-card text note:** the Neeker-breekers card used by the Challenge Deck list is the *The Wizards* version. Its printed effect says that each character faces one strike at prowess equal to that character's mind; a character who would be wounded is only tapped, with no body check. The card's erratum also says it does not affect Wizards. Do not apply the Minion-side card wording to this Hero teaching deck.

### Twilight is conditional in the first-play build

The active 36-card Hero main deck contains **no Environment Resource**. The Hero sideboard has Gates of Morning, which is an Environment, but the sideboard is kept closed for Game 1 and the first fixed-deck scoring trial. Therefore, unless another Environment card enters play, a drawn **Twilight has no legal target** in the baseline game. Do not play it against Fellowship, Full of Froth and Rage, Wake of War or another non-Environment event. Under the Academy's solo handling, an unplayable drawn Twilight is discarded with the other unused Hazards at the end of the phase. It becomes useful when a later campaign brings an Environment such as Gates of Morning into play.

In this solo setup, a Twilight drawn from the separate Hazard deck is used only as a Hazard. Although the card can be played as a Resource in normal two-player deck construction, that option is not available to a card drawn from this Academy Hazard deck.

### Candidate for later tuning — Arouse Minions

A non-Dragon option worth testing **after** the baseline games is **Arouse Minions** (The Wizards). It is a Hazard short-event playable on a Shadow-hold or Dark-hold and increases one automatic attack at that site by +3 prowess for the turn. Moria and Goblin-gate are both Shadow-holds, so it would add a direct, route-relevant threat to their Orc automatic attacks without adding a Dragon creature.

A controlled first experiment would be **one Arouse Minions replacing one Twilight**, leaving one Twilight available for future Environment cards. This is only a proposed v1.1 test, not part of the locked 30-card list. Do not change the baseline list during the first three recorded games, and do not buy the card just for this test until your physical collection has been checked. If you later test it, count it against the Hazard Limit and remember that the Hero may choose not to enter the site.

Keep this Hazard deck separate from the 36-card Hero play deck, Hero discard pile, Location deck and sideboard.

That is not automatically a flaw: the deck is a historical teaching list and the first three routes are not the whole map. It is, however, something to record during playtesting rather than ignore.

## What the Hazard deck teaches

The Hazard deck is the opponent's toolbox. In solo play, you control it during the Hero company's Movement/Hazard Phase. Your task is to choose the most effective **legal** pressure, not to invent extra rules or target something merely because it looks dramatic.

The Challenge Deck C mix gives repeated practice with:

- **Creature legality and keying:** Cave Worm, Giant Spiders, Lesser Spiders, Neeker-breekers, Wargs and Watcher in the Water.
- **Attack amplification:** Full of Froth and Rage and Wake of War, subject to their exact card text and timing.
- **Disruption and roadblocks:** Despair of the Heart, River and Seized by Terror.
- **Environment interaction:** Twilight.
- **Sequencing:** deciding which legal card should be played first and what should follow.

This is a teaching structure, not a substitute for reading the printed wording of an individual card.

### Despair of the Heart: pressure on the Book bearer

Despair of the Heart is not merely a one-off two-point penalty. It is a **Corruption** permanent-event. It can be played on a non-Wizard, non-Hobbit character; the target receives 2 corruption points and makes a corruption check each time a character in his company becomes wounded. It cannot be duplicated on the same character. During his Organization Phase, the target may tap to try to remove it with a roll greater than 4.

For the Dwarven Quest company, all five starting Dwarves are eligible. If a non-Wizard Dwarf carries Book of Mazarbul, targeting that bearer can turn every wound suffered by anyone in the company into a corruption-check trigger for the bearer. Kíli is especially vulnerable because his -1 corruption-check modifier is a penalty. If Gandalf carries the Book, Despair of the Heart cannot target him because he is a Wizard; the Hazard player must choose another eligible non-Wizard, non-Hobbit character, and the repeated checks apply to that target.

**Hazard-player rule:** choose the target whose corruption burden and company role create the greatest real pressure. The Book bearer is often the strongest target once the Book is in play, but only if the bearer is a legal target: Despair cannot be played on a Wizard. Check the actual bearer, current corruption points, existing corruption cards, and whether the target can remove the card. Only one corruption card may be played on a given character per turn.

**Hero-player response:** when the target gets an Organization Phase, decide whether to tap him to attempt removal before relying on the Book-storage check. If the card remains, include its 2 CP in the total and recalculate any support needed. Tapping the target to attempt removal is not the same as tapping other characters to support a corruption check.

On **Moria → Goblin-gate** (Redhorn Gate → Hollin → Rhudaur → High Pass), all four regions are Wilderness. The route still makes the Wilderness-keyed creatures candidates and includes Redhorn Gate and High Pass for Cave Worm. Watcher in the Water can use its double-Wilderness key; its special permission to be played at Moria does not apply because Goblin-gate is the new site. On **Goblin-gate → Blue Mountain Dwarf-hold** (High Pass → Rhudaur → Arthedain → Númeriador), all four regions are also Wilderness. This is the practical test of fair solo play: the Hazard side should exploit every legal key and target, but must not treat a Shadow-hold site as a Shadow-land region or play a card whose condition is absent.

## Three numbers and rules that must not be confused

### 1. Solo Hazard draw

The Academy uses the solo procedure described on the [Solo Rules page]({{ '/reference/solo-ruleset/' | relative_url }}): draw **2 + twice the normal Hazard draw number for the destination site**.

For the Game 1 move to Moria, the normal Hazard draw number is 3, so the solo draw is:

**2 + (2 × 3) = 8 Hazard cards.**

This tells you how many cards are available to the solo Hazard player for that company's Movement/Hazard Phase. First identify the site whose normal draw number applies under the movement rules; for a move to a Haven, normal card draws are based on the site of origin.

### 2. Base Hazard Limit

The normal base Hazard Limit is the company's size or two, whichever is greater. **Each Hobbit or Orc scout counts as half a character, with the total rounded up; the minimum limit is two.** It is set for that company's Movement/Hazard Phase and remains fixed for that phase, even if the company later loses a character. Other applicable rules or card effects can modify it; check the chosen scenario before using a special case.

For the five-character starting company in Game 1, the base Hazard Limit is **5**. If you later change the company to include Hobbits or Orc scouts, recalculate the limit using the half-character rule instead of simply counting character cards.

### 3. The solo procedure for playing the drawn Hazards

The solo procedure directs you to play **as many of the drawn Hazards as you can within the Hazard Limit**, choosing the combination that is most disadvantageous for your Hero company. Eight cards drawn does not mean eight ordinary limit-counting plays: with a base limit of five, no more than five Hazard cards that count against that limit can be played in the phase.

**Important exception — Twilight:** the Challenge Deck C list contains two copies. Twilight may be played at any point during either player's turn and **does not count against the Hazard Limit**. As a Hazard, it cancels and discards one Environment card in play or declared earlier in the same chain of effects. It is still a Hazard otherwise, and it may also be played as a Resource. So a legal Twilight does not use one of the company's five ordinary Hazard slots. Check its target and timing; it is not a free creature attack.

Do not automatically play the first five cards. Check legality and sequence, then choose the strongest legal combination. Do not deliberately leave a useful legal slot empty merely because a card seems less dramatic. If you are pausing for a teaching discussion, pause before committing the play and explain the competing choices.

## A site automatic attack is not a Hazard play

Moria's four Orc strikes at 7 prowess and Goblin-gate's three Orc strikes at 6 prowess are **site automatic attacks**. They are faced in the Site Phase if the company chooses to enter. They do not consume Hazard Limit slots.

A Hazard card that legally modifies or adds to an automatic attack is still a Hazard play and normally counts against the Hazard Limit. The Hazard side may play a legal effect targeting an automatic attack at the company's new site during the Movement/Hazard Phase, even if the Hero player later declines to enter. Check the card's exact target and timing.

The Hero version of Blue Mountain Dwarf-hold is a Free-hold with a normal Hazard draw of 1 and **no automatic attack**. Do not import the different Minion site-card data into this Hero deck.

Practise the separate arrival and entry decisions in the [Site Entry & Automatic Attack Lab]({{ '/scenarios/site-entry-lab/' | relative_url }}), with a separate [model answer]({{ '/scenarios/site-entry-lab/answer-key/' | relative_url }}).

## The seven-step Hazard decision

1. **Legality:** can the card be played against this company, its site path, destination site or another legal target?
2. **Key:** if it is a creature, which region type, region name, site type or site name is being used to key it?
3. **Objective:** what is the company trying to achieve this expedition?
4. **Critical character:** whose disruption would most damage that objective?
5. **Sequence:** does the wording of another card make this play stronger before or after it?
6. **Limit:** how many Hazard cards that count against the limit have already been declared?
7. **Best combination:** which legal set of plays creates the greatest disadvantage within the remaining limit?

For double-region keying, check that the company site path contains the required number of matching regions; matching regions need not be consecutive. Check the actual card text and current official rulings before resolving an unusual case.

## Route-first Hazard hand audit

Before selecting a combination, put the declared site path beside the drawn Hazard hand. Sort candidates into these groups, then check the printed wording:

| Group | First check | Decision to make |
|---|---|---|
| **Named-region / site threats** | Is the required region name or destination site actually part of this company's situation? | Keep only if the printed key and every other condition are satisfied. |
| **Region-type creatures** | Does the declared site path contain the required region type(s), including the required count for double/triple keys? | Count the actual path; do not count a region merely because it appears on a map nearby. |
| **Events and support** | Is the target, timing window, condition and duration valid now? | Determine whether it disrupts the mission or improves a legal attack, rather than assuming it combines with every creature. |

For the Academy's Rivendell → Moria example, the Region Movement path Rhudaur → Hollin → Redhorn Gate contains three Wilderness regions and names Redhorn Gate; Moria is the destination site. That makes several cards in the teaching list worth checking, but it does not make every card automatically legal. The [Route Risk Workshop]({{ '/scenarios/route-risk-workshop/' | relative_url }}) gives you a repeatable way to compare routes and record what the actual Hazard hand permits. The [Hero Resource Timing Workshop]({{ '/scenarios/hero-resource-practice/' | relative_url }}) also teaches how the starting Dwarves' skills affect the response to River, Concealment and the Moria attack.

After legality, compare impact and sequence. Count each play against the Hazard Limit according to the normal rules and the card's exact wording, and respect any explicit exception. If you cannot prove an interaction from the physical text or a current ruling, mark it **unverified** and do not rely on it for the play.

## Worked example — Moria

The Hero company of five characters moves to Moria.

- Solo Hazard draw: **8 cards**.
- Base Hazard Limit: **5**.
- Available plays: only cards that are legal and meet their printed requirements.
- Selection: choose the most disadvantageous legal combination, respecting sequencing and the five-card limit.

A card that cannot be legally keyed is not an option, however powerful it looks. A legal event may be more damaging to the expedition than a creature, depending on the company's current state and the card wording. Do not decide by attack prowess alone.

- **Watcher in the Water** can be considered through its double-Wilderness key on these legs. Its special permission to be played at Moria is not the reason when the destination is Goblin-gate or Blue Mountain.
- **Goblin-gate's Orc automatic attack** (three strikes at 6 prowess) is a separate site encounter. It happens only if the company enters; it is not one of the six drawn Hazards or one of the five Hazard Limit slots.
- **Blue Mountain Dwarf-hold** is a Free-hold by site type but is the named Dwarf-hold required by Book of Mazarbul. Its Hero site card has no automatic attack, but the company still faces the four-card solo Hazard draw on arrival.

The figures assume the normal Challenge Deck C site cards and a five-character company with no relevant modifiers. Recalculate the Hazard Limit if the company changes. The workbook and separate [model answer]({{ '/scenarios/book-journey/answer-key/' | relative_url }}) give the card-by-card exercise.

## Physical construction

1. Find the 30 listed Hazard cards and check each quantity.
2. Shuffle them thoroughly.
3. Place the Hazard deck on the opposite side of the table from the Hero deck.
4. Mark a separate Hazard discard pile.
5. Keep any Hazard sideboard cards separate.
6. Do not reveal or pre-arrange the draw order.
7. During each solo Movement/Hazard Phase, draw according to the selected solo procedure, then apply the normal Hazard Limit and card legality.

## After each Hazard phase: Academy discard convention and recycling

- Under the Academy's selected supplementary procedure, Hazard cards drawn but not played are discarded at the end of that Movement/Hazard Phase. This handling follows [Simon Cogan / Dragoncymru's My Solo Rules](https://meccg.wordpress.com/my-solo-rules/); the printed solitaire paragraph gives the draw/play formula but does not state this extra discard step.
- When the Hazard deck is exhausted and more cards must be drawn, shuffle the Hazard discard pile to form a new Hazard deck.
- In this Book of Mazarbul mission, non-unique defeated creatures go to the Hazard discard so they can return when the deck is recycled. Follow the normal rules for unique creatures; this mission does not award kill MPs as its victory objective.
- Keep the Hero play-deck discard and Hazard discard separate. Do not return unused drawn Hazards straight to the deck.

## Multi-company solo play — resolve each company separately

The solitaire draw formula applies **for each company**, not once for the whole turn. Each company takes its own Movement/Hazard Phase, and each phase has its own route, target checks and Hazard Limit. If a company moves to a Haven, normal rules use the Hazard draw number of its **site of origin**; if it moves to a non-Haven, use the new site's number.

### Worked two-company example

The Academy's Gandalf-led adaptation uses a support-company example. The historical Challenge Deck C strategy is Pallando-based. If he is in play and moves separately from the five Dwarves:

| Company | Movement | Normal Hazard draw number | Solo draw | Base Hazard Limit |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Thorin's five Dwarves | Rivendell → Moria | Moria 3 | 8 | 5 |
| Gandalf alone | Grey Havens → Lórien | Grey Havens 2 (Lórien is a Haven) | 6 | 2 |
| **Total** | Two separate phases | — | **14** | **Separate limits: 5 and 2** |

The 14 cards come from the same 30-card Hazard deck, but they are not one combined draw and do not create a combined Hazard Limit of seven. Resolve the Dwarves' phase and Gandalf's phase separately. Choose the strongest legal opposition for each company, using that company's actual site path, targets, condition and limit.

If Gandalf joins the Dwarves instead, the company has six characters: the Moria draw remains eight, but the base Hazard Limit rises to six. The Wizard is also exposed to the Dwarves' Moria journey and automatic attack.

Gandalf's +1 corruption-check modifier is a separate character effect. It does not change the five-card Hero hand target, Hazard draw or Hazard Limit.

See [Gandalf and the Support Company]({{ '/decks/gandalf-support-company/' | relative_url }}) and the [Gandalf company practice scenario]({{ '/scenarios/gandalf-company/' | relative_url }}) for the full decision and model answer.

## Solo play: a fair test, not an easy test

Before testing card combinations, complete the [Hazard Draw & Limit Lab]({{ '/scenarios/hazard-draw-limit-lab/' | relative_url }}). It separates the site card's normal Hazard draw number, the solitaire draw formula, the company's base Hazard Limit, and card effects that modify that limit.

The learner controls both sides, so use a consistent rule: the Hazard side should make the strongest legal choices available under the selected solo procedure. Do not give the Hero side hidden assistance or use knowledge that is not available from the rules and revealed cards. If a decision is unclear, stop and check the card text or rules before resolving it.
