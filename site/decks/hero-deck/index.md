## Resource-to-site plan — what the deck is travelling to do

The Challenge Deck C player aid gives the following suggested destinations for its key resources. Treat these as a **target map**, not a mandatory route: check the physical site card, its automatic attack, the resource's printed play conditions, and the company's current condition before committing to a trip.

| Resource objective | Suggested site(s) in the historical player aid | Captain's planning note |
|---|---|---|
| **Book of Mazarbul** | **Moria** | This is the Game 1 objective. The follow-up campaign can aim to store it at Blue Mountain Dwarf-hold, but that is a later expedition. |
| **Bow of Dragon-horn** | **Ovir Hollow**, or the player aid's Bounty of the Hoard line | Check the exact conditions and compare the site's danger with the benefit of the item. |
| **Magical Harp** | **Ovir Hollow**, or the player aid's Bounty of the Hoard line | Do not make the trip just because the card is in hand; decide whether it improves the current mission. |

| **Hauberk of Bright Mail** | **Sarn Goriwing** or **Buhr Widu** | Armour may help later, but reaching it while already weakened can defeat the purpose. |

## The first campaign arc

| Stage | Mission | Route lesson | Do not assume |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Game 1** | Reach Moria and acquire Book of Mazarbul. | Rivendell → Moria via Rhudaur → Hollin → Redhorn Gate is a legal three-region teaching route, after checking adjacency. | Arriving at Moria does not mean the company must enter regardless of wounds and taps. |
| **Reassess** | Decide whether to recover, consolidate, or continue. | Judge the actual arrival state before declaring another destination. | The original plan does not automatically remain best after the objective changes. |
| **Later campaign** | Store the Book at Blue Mountain Dwarf-hold and consider the faction. | The direct Moria-to-Blue-Mountain sequence has five regions, so split it into legal legs unless a specific effect changes the limit. | Do not treat the sideboard faction as if it were already in the Hero play deck. |
| **Optional side objective** | Collect another item or ally. | Compare its site, route exposure, reward, and next position with the main mission. | A card in hand is not a command to travel to its site immediately. |

This keeps the Hero deck practical: first learn one complete expedition, then use the historical resource map to choose the next objective deliberately.

## First: what you are actually building

For the Academy, think of the Hero setup as **several separate components**, not one giant deck.

1. **Location deck** — sites available for movement.
2. **Hero play deck** — the 30 Hero resources for the teaching game.
3. **Character pool** — possible starting/recruitable characters.
4. **Sideboard** — advanced reserve cards.
5. **Hero discard pile** — cards that leave the play deck during the game.

The location deck is separate from the shuffled play deck.

For **Game 1**, do not add cards just because you own them. Use the exact teaching list above so the lessons and examples match what is on the table.

## Test before changing the list

Use the [Expedition Decision Lab]({{ '/scenarios/expedition-decision-lab/' | relative_url }}) to practise choosing and retaining resources for a real expedition. Then use the [Solo Playtest Protocol]({{ '/scenarios/solo-playtest/' | relative_url }}) for three repeatable games before changing card quantities. A card not drawn is not evidence that it is weak; record when it is useful, situational or genuinely unusable.

## Pilot's guide — what to learn from the 30-card list

Keep the verified 30-card list unchanged for the first lessons. Use the functions below as a learning aid, not as a replacement for the printed card text.

| Function | Cards to notice | Decision to practise |
|---|---|---|
| Avoid an unnecessary fight | Concealment; Not at Home; Hundreds of Butterflies | Is preserving the company more valuable than fighting this attack? |
| Protect the expedition | Fellowship; Marvels Told | What danger matters now, and what can this resource legally answer? |
| Improve movement or future options | Great-road; Thror's Map; Smoke Rings | Does playing this now improve the current trip or preserve a stronger later option? |
| Make one expedition count | Bounty of the Hoard; Lucky Search | Is the opportunity worth the risk and the resources spent to reach it? |
| Fight when the objective demands it | Risky Blow; The Dwarves Are Upon You!; the weapon and armour items | Which character should take the risk, and what happens if the fight goes badly? |
| Build long-term value | Book of Mazarbul; Iron Hill Dwarves; Men of Dorwinion; allies and items | What does this acquisition contribute to the mission and the campaign? |
| Recruit and develop | A Friend or Three | Can the company legally and usefully add a character without weakening the current plan? |

### The hand-management test

Before discarding a card, classify it as one of three things:

1. **Needed now:** supports the next movement, site attempt or defence.
2. **Needed later:** depends on a future site, company state or objective.
3. **Low value for this plan:** unlikely to help the current campaign line.

Do not discard a card just because it is not playable this turn. Equally, do not keep every card indefinitely: the next expedition should determine what is worth holding.

### Deck integrity rule

The list remains the historical Challenge Deck C teaching baseline. Do not quietly replace cards or quantities while using Game 1 and its workbook; otherwise the scripted examples stop matching the physical deck. Record any future alternative as a separate revision with its own list and test games.

## How to physically construct it

1. Find the 30 resource cards listed above.
2. Check every quantity.
3. Put those Hero resources into one pile.
4. Keep the starting characters separate until setup.
5. Keep the other characters separate.
6. Assemble the 15 site cards as the Location deck.
7. Keep the sideboard separate.
8. Check that no Hazard cards have been mixed into the Hero resource deck.
9. Shuffle the 30-card Hero resource deck.
10. Do **not** shuffle the Location deck.

## Physical table layout

Put the Hero resource deck on the left.

Place the Hero discard immediately beside it.

Place the current company in the centre.

Keep the Location deck above the company.

Keep the character pool and sideboard off to the side.

Keep the Hazard deck and Hazard discard on the opposite side of the table.

Do not mix any of these piles.

## Hand management

Your hand is not a second discard pile.

Cards in hand are resources you may need later.

Before discarding a card, ask:

> Is this card useless now, or merely not useful yet?

This is particularly important for cards such as **Thror's Map**, whose value may be future expedition compression.

## Starting company versus character pool

Do not confuse the **starting company** with the entire character portion of the deck.

The company is the group actually beginning the game.

The remaining characters are future recruitment/development options.

That distinction becomes important as soon as the Academy moves beyond Game 1.

## Collection-building rule

Do not buy a card simply because it is “good.”

Buy it because it fills a known Academy role:

- required for Game 1;
- improves the core Hero deck;
- enables a later route/objective;
- teaches an important mechanic;
- or expands the campaign.

This keeps genuine-card acquisition focused and prevents the collection from becoming the project.
