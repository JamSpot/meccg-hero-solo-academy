# Hero Deck Construction — Beginner Guide

## The exact teaching card list

The 30-card table below is the **Resource portion**, not the whole shuffled Hero play deck. ## Book of Mazarbul — what the expedition is actually securing

The current card database gives Book of Mazarbul these important facts:

- **Unique Special Item**, with **2 corruption points**.
- **Only playable at Moria**.
- Worth **1 marshalling point while in play**.
- It may be stored at a **Dwarf-hold for 5 marshalling points**.
- If its bearer is a **Sage**, you may tap it during your Organization Phase to increase your hand size by one until your next Untap Phase.

The five starting Dwarves in this teaching company have Warrior, Scout, Ranger and Diplomat skills, but **none is a Sage**. Do not plan on using the Book's hand-size ability during Game 1. That ability becomes relevant only if a Sage later bears the Book and you can legally use its effect.

This creates two distinct objectives:

1. **Game 1 — ACQUIRE:** reach Moria and play Book of Mazarbul if the company can safely complete the site attempt.
2. **Later campaign — STORE:** move the Book to a Dwarf-hold, such as Blue Mountain Dwarf-hold, to realise its 5 MP stored value.

The second objective is a new expedition, not an automatic continuation of the first. The direct Moria-to-Blue-Mountain route contains five regions, so it cannot be completed in one turn under the normal four-region limit. You do **not** have to use a Haven as the intermediate stop: the fixed 15-site deck contains Goblin-gate, allowing a two-leg non-Haven route (Moria → Goblin-gate → Blue Mountain Dwarf-hold).

## The first campaign arc

| Stage | Mission | Route lesson | Do not assume |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Game 1** | Reach Moria and acquire Book of Mazarbul. | Rivendell → Moria via Rhudaur → Hollin → Redhorn Gate is a legal three-region teaching route, after checking adjacency. | Arriving at Moria does not mean the company must enter regardless of wounds and taps. |
| **Reassess** | Decide whether to recover, consolidate, or continue. | Judge the actual arrival state before declaring another destination. | The original plan does not automatically remain best after the objective changes. |
| **Later campaign** | Store the Book at Blue Mountain Dwarf-hold and consider the faction. | Use two legal non-Haven legs: Moria → Goblin-gate (Redhorn Gate → Hollin → Rhudaur → High Pass), then Goblin-gate → Blue Mountain Dwarf-hold (High Pass → Rhudaur → Arthedain → Númeriador). Each leg is four Wilderness regions. | Goblin-gate can be a waypoint without being entered. If you enter to attempt Gollum, face its three Orc strikes at 6 prowess; if you do not enter, you avoid the site automatic attack but cannot play cards or take other site-phase actions there. |
| **Optional side objective** | Collect another item or ally. | Compare its site, route exposure, reward, and next position with the main mission. | A card in hand is not a command to travel to its site immediately. |

This keeps the Hero deck practical: first learn one complete expedition, then use the historical resource map to choose the next objective deliberately. The no-Haven route is an advanced follow-up, not a shortcut around Hazard exposure: the solo draw is six cards on the Goblin-gate leg and four on the Blue Mountain leg. Blue Mountain Dwarf-hold has no automatic attack and a normal Hazard draw of one. Its site-type symbol is Free-hold [{F}], but it is still the named Dwarf-hold required by the Book's storage text. Store the Book during the Organization Phase while the company is at the site, after the bearer's corruption check.

## First: what you are actually building

For the Academy, think of the Hero setup as **several separate components**, not one giant deck.

1. **Location deck** — sites available for movement.
2. **Hero play deck** — 30 Hero Resources plus the eight additional character cards listed above: **38 cards total**, with no Hazards.
3. **Starting pool** — the five starting Dwarves and the two starting Cram items.
4. **Sideboard** — advanced reserve cards.
5. **Hero discard pile** — cards that leave the play deck during the game.

The location deck is separate from the shuffled play deck.

For **Game 1**, do not add cards just because you own them. Use the exact 30 Resource cards and eight additional character cards above, so the 38-card Hero play deck and the lessons match what is on the table.

## Test before changing the list

Use the [Expedition Decision Lab]({{ '/scenarios/expedition-decision-lab/' | relative_url }}) to practise choosing and retaining resources for a real expedition. ## What the teaching deck is trying to do

The 30 Resources are not interchangeable. Learn who can use them, when they can be played, and what price or restriction comes with the effect. The same card can be excellent on one expedition and irrelevant on another.

### Starting-company skill map

The five starting Dwarves have a useful spread of skills. All five are Warriors; two are Scouts; one is a Ranger; none is a Sage.

| Starting character | Skills | What this means in play |
|---|---|---|
| **Thorin II** | Warrior / Scout / Diplomat | Can use Warrior-only combat resources and Scout-only actions such as Concealment or Lucky Search. |
| **Dori** | Warrior | Can use Warrior-only combat resources; his own card text helps against Orcs but penalises faction influence. |
| **Kíli** | Warrior / Scout | Your second starting Scout; can use Concealment or Lucky Search when their conditions are met. |
| **Glóin** | Warrior / Diplomat | Can use Warrior-only combat resources and has useful Dwarf-influence bonuses. |
| **Óin** | Warrior / Ranger | Can use Warrior-only combat resources and can use Ranger actions, including cancelling River if he is able to tap at the start of the site phase. |

**Important:** the starting company has no Sage. Do not plan to use Marvels Told until a Sage is actually in the company. A Wizard or later character is not automatically in the starting group just because a copy is in the play deck.

### Card roles, timing and limitations

| Card | What it actually does | What to check before using it |
|---|---|---|
| **A Friend or Three** | Gives one influence check **or** one corruption check by a character in the company +1 for each character in that company. With the five starting Dwarves, that is +5. | It modifies one check, not every check. Save it for a check that matters. |
| **Fellowship** | At a Haven, during the Organization Phase, it can be played on a company with at least four characters and allies. It gives all of them +1 prowess and +1 to corruption checks. | It is discarded if a character or ally joins or leaves that company. The five Dwarves qualify at Rivendell, but adding a new member later breaks the Fellowship. |
| **Concealment** | Scout only: tap the Scout to cancel one attack against his company. | Thorin II and Kíli are the starting Scouts. It cancels one attack, not every attack in the phase; tapping the Scout is a real cost. |
| **Not at Home** | Cancels one Dragon, Drake or Troll attack if it is an automatic attack or keyed to a site. Alternatively, if Gates of Morning is in play, it reduces the strikes of any automatic attack by two, to a minimum of one. | It is not a universal attack-cancel card. Moria's automatic attack is Orcs, so Not at Home cannot cancel it. Gates of Morning is in the sideboard, not the 30-card main Resource list. |
| **The Dwarves Are Upon You!** | When a company containing Dwarves faces an attack, all Dwarves in that company receive +2 prowess and -1 body against that attack. | It helps the whole Dwarf company, but the -1 body is a real risk. It cannot be duplicated against the same attack. |
| **Risky Blow** | Warrior only, against one strike: +3 prowess and -1 body. | All five starting Dwarves are Warriors. This modifies one strike, not the whole attack; the lower body can make a successful strike more dangerous. |
| **Hundreds of Butterflies** | During a moving character's Movement/Hazard Phase, it untaps that character and increases the Hazard Limit against the company by one. | It does **not** reduce the Hazard draw. With a base limit of five, it can allow the Hazard side a sixth limit-counting play. Use it only when readying the character is worth giving the opposition that extra slot. |
| **Marvels Told** | Sage only: tap a Sage to discard a Hazard non-Environment permanent-event or long-event; the Sage then makes a corruption check modified by -2. | No starting Dwarf is a Sage. It can answer effects such as Full of Froth and Rage or Wake of War, but not short-events such as River or Seized by Terror, and not an Environment card. |
| **Lucky Search** | Scout only, during the site phase, at a tapped or untapped Shadow-hold or Dark-hold. Reveal play-deck cards until a non-special item is found or the deck ends; the Scout takes control of it if found, then faces one uncancellable strike with prowess 3 plus the number of cards revealed. | Moria and Goblin-gate are Shadow-holds, so the site condition can be met there. The item is discarded if the Scout is wounded. Book of Mazarbul is a Special Item, so Lucky Search is not a way to find the Book. |
| **Bounty of the Hoard** | During the site phase, it allows one minor or major item to be played at a tapped site that contains a hoard. | A tapped site is not automatically a hoard site. Check the site and item conditions; this is an extra item opportunity, not deck compression. |
| **Thrór's Map** | A unique minor item that can be discarded to untap a site with a Dragon automatic attack. | It is a site-untap tool, not card draw. Moria and Goblin-gate have Orc automatic attacks, not Dragon attacks, so the Map does not help at either site. |
| **Great-road** | A short-event played at the end of the Organization Phase on a company at a Haven. The opponent may draw up to twice the normal number of cards for that company during its Movement/Hazard Phase; at the end of the turn the company may return to the Haven where it began, with no second Movement/Hazard Phase. | This is a special return-to-Haven effect, **not** the general Region Movement rule and not a way to ignore route legality. The published solitaire draw formula does not spell out how Great-road's doubled draw interacts with its own 2 + twice-normal formula, so keep it out of the baseline Game 1 until that interaction is resolved. |
| **Smoke Rings** | Moves one Resource or Character from the sideboard or discard pile into the play deck, then shuffles. | It puts the card into the deck, not directly into your hand. Use it as recovery/selection for a later draw. |

### The actual learning sequence

**PLAN → PREPARE → AVOID → FIGHT → ACQUIRE → RECOVER → REASSESS**

- **Plan:** choose the mission and route before committing to a site.
- **Prepare:** use Haven-only resources such as Fellowship at the correct time.
- **Avoid:** preserve a Scout and Concealment for a genuinely dangerous attack; do not assume Not at Home works against every creature.
- **Fight:** use the Dwarf-wide bonus or a one-strike bonus only when the actual attack justifies the body risk.
- **Acquire:** attempt the Book, item, ally or faction that supports the current mission.
- **Recover:** use site-specific item effects and Smoke Rings only when their conditions are met.
- **Reassess:** after the site phase, decide whether to recover, continue or change the next objective.

### The hand-management test

Before discarding a card, classify it as one of three things:

1. **Needed now:** supports the next movement, site attempt or defence.
2. **Needed later:** depends on a future site, company state or objective.
3. **Low value for this plan:** unlikely to help the current campaign line.

Do not discard a card just because it is not playable this turn. Equally, do not keep every card indefinitely: the next expedition should determine what is worth holding.

### Deck integrity rule

The list remains the historical Challenge Deck C teaching baseline. Do not quietly replace cards or quantities while using Game 1 and its workbook; otherwise the scripted examples stop matching the physical deck. Record any future alternative as a separate revision with its own list and test games.

## How to physically construct it

1. Find the 30 Hero Resource cards listed above and check every quantity.
2. Find the eight additional character cards listed above.
3. Shuffle those 38 cards together to form the **Hero play deck**. Do not put Hazards in it.
4. Keep the five starting characters and two Cram items ready as the starting pool/setup cards.
5. Assemble the 15 site cards as the Location deck; keep it separate and do **not** shuffle it.
6. Keep the 20-card sideboard separate.
7. Mark a separate Hero discard pile and a separate Hazard discard pile.
8. Shuffle the 30-card Hazard deck separately.

## Physical table layout

Put the 38-card Hero play deck on the left.

Place the Hero discard immediately beside it.

Place the current company in the centre.

Keep the Location deck above the company.

Keep the starting pool and sideboard off to the side.

Keep the Hazard deck and Hazard discard on the opposite side of the table.

Do not mix any of these piles.

## Hand management

Your hand is not a second discard pile.

Cards in hand are resources you may need later.

Before discarding a card, ask:

> Is this card useless now, or merely not useful yet?

This is particularly important for cards such as **Thror's Map**, whose value may be future expedition compression.

## Starting company versus additional characters

Do not confuse the **starting company** with the eight additional character cards in the Hero play deck.

The starting company is Thorin II, Dori, Kíli, Glóin and Óin, with the two Cram items assigned as listed above. The other eight characters are shuffled into the Hero play deck. They are drawn and may be played later when their printed home-site/Haven requirements and the normal influence and company rules allow it. They are not a second starting company and are not a separate pool to draw from.

## Plan the Hero hand by mission stage

The teaching deck is not a pile of cards that are all equally urgent. Before moving, label each useful card by the decision it supports. This is a planning aid; the printed text determines whether a card can actually be played.

| Mission stage | Ask yourself | Common mistake to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| **Before movement** | Which card improves this trip, which protects against a likely problem, and which only matters at a later site? | Keeping every card “just in case” without a named future use. |
| **During movement / Hazards** | Is this resource legal now, and does it address the threat that is actually present? | Spending a defensive resource on a hypothetical threat while ignoring the current state. |
| **At the destination** | What does the objective require, and what resources or characters are still available after Hazards? | Treating arrival as an instruction to attempt the site regardless of wounds and taps. |
| **After the objective** | Which cards support the next planned leg, recovery, or a new objective? | Continuing the original route after the mission has changed. |

### The three-question keep test

For each card you are tempted to keep, finish these sentences:

1. **I am keeping this because…**
2. **I expect to use it when…**
3. **If that situation does not arise, I will reconsider it when…**

This prevents two opposite errors: discarding a situational card before its use matters, and hoarding cards with no plan. Do not infer a card's effect from its title; check the physical card text and the current rules.

## Opening-hand practice — plan the mission, not just the cards

Use this short exercise before Game 1, then repeat it after you have played once. Your solitaire hand size is five cards.

**Mission:** start at Rivendell and plan the expedition to acquire Book of Mazarbul at Moria.

1. **Name the objective.** Book of Mazarbul is the reason for this trip; do not let an optional item or ally automatically replace the mission.
2. **Sort the five-card hand.** Mark each card as (a) movement/planning, (b) avoid or survive a threat, (c) fight if needed, (d) a site-specific or later objective, or (e) uncertain until you read the card.
3. **Check the actual text.** A card's category does not prove it is playable now. Check its timing, target, site and any conditions.
4. **Keep a credible fallback.** Do not spend every defensive option on an imagined threat before you know what the Hazard side actually plays; equally, do not save a card that is the correct legal answer to a real threat.
5. **Plan the next hand.** After movement, site resolution and the normal hand adjustment for the selected solo rules, decide what you need for the next expedition rather than hoarding everything.

### Worked sorting example

Imagine your hand contains **Great-road, Concealment, Book of Mazarbul, Risky Blow, and Lucky Search**. Do not assume all five should be played immediately:

| Card | First question | Planning category |
|---|---|---|
| Great-road | Is the company at a Haven at the end of Organization, and is the extra Hazard draw worth a special return to that Haven? | Special return-to-Haven option; not general free movement |
| Concealment | Is a Scout available to tap to cancel one attack? Thorin II and Kíli are Scouts. | One-attack response; tapping the Scout is the cost |
| Book of Mazarbul | Is the company at the right site, and can the resource be played under its printed conditions? | Current mission objective |
| Risky Blow | Is there a relevant attack, and is the risk worth taking for the objective? | Fight only when justified |
| Lucky Search | Is the company at a Shadow-hold or Dark-hold, and is the Scout worth risking on its uncancellable search strike? | Risky site-phase item search; it cannot find the Special Item Book |

This is a **card-reading exercise, not a scripted play sequence**. Use the text on your physical cards to decide what is legal. If a card is not useful now, record whether it has a credible later use before discarding it.

Once you have a route in mind, test the other half of the decision: complete the [Hero Resource Timing Workshop]({{ '/scenarios/hero-resource-practice/' | relative_url }}) and then the [Hazard Legality Workshop]({{ '/scenarios/hazard-legality-workshop/' | relative_url }}) to see how the route affects the Hazard hand you must face. The aim is to plan a Hero hand that supports a mission while still making honest allowance for the opposition.

## Collection-building rule

Do not buy a card simply because it is “good.”

Buy it because it fills a known Academy role:

- required for Game 1;
- improves the core Hero deck;
- enables a later route/objective;
- teaches an important mechanic;
- or expands the campaign.

This keeps genuine-card acquisition focused and prevents the collection from becoming the project.
