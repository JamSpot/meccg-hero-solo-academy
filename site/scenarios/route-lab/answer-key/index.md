# Route Lab — Model Answers

Use these after completing the [Route Lab workbook]({{ '/scenarios/route-lab/' | relative_url }}). The model answers explain the process; actual card text and current rulings take precedence.

## 1 — Plan now, declare the site path later

During Organization, declare the destination and ensure the intended movement can be legal. During the Movement/Hazard Phase, reveal the new site. Then declare the Region Movement site path. If the movement is illegal when the site is revealed, it is negated and the company remains at its origin, while still conducting its Movement/Hazard Phase.

## 2 — Three Wilderness regions

The route names Rhudaur, Hollin and Redhorn Gate; each is a Wilderness region. Before playing a double-Wilderness creature, verify that the creature's printed keying permits the play, that the site path contains at least two matching regions, that the creature is otherwise legal, and that the timing and Hazard Limit permit it. The matching regions need not be consecutive.

## 3 — The tempting five-region trip

It is not legal as one normal Region Movement: the route has five regions including origin and destination. Find a legal intermediate site and make a second movement on a later turn, unless a specific card effect changes the normal limit. A teaching example is Moria → Rivendell via Redhorn Gate → Hollin → Rhudaur, followed by Rivendell → Blue Mountain Dwarf-hold via Rhudaur → Arthedain → Númeriador. Verify each adjacency against your region cards.

## 4 — Same destination, different route

Compare at least: (1) legal movement type; (2) region count and adjacency; (3) region types and names exposed; (4) likely legal creature Hazards; (5) site reward and automatic attack; (6) company's likely arrival condition; (7) next-turn position; (8) recovery options. No route wins just because it is shorter.

## 5 — Eight drawn Hazards, five slots

The solo procedure makes eight Hazard cards available. The five-character company has a base Hazard Limit of five. Evaluate all eight, check legality and timing, then play as many as possible within the limit using the most disadvantageous legal combination. Do not play illegal cards or exceed the limit.

## 6 — The legal but low-impact Hazard

First remove the illegal card from consideration. Check the three legal cards' targets, keying, printed requirements and timing. Determine whether the amplifier actually improves another legal play and whether the order is valid. Under the selected solo procedure, choose the most disadvantageous legal combination within the remaining Hazard slots.

## 7 — The damaged expedition

Check the automatic attack and site requirements, who can still fight, the company's wounds and taps, available avoidance/defence, the value and urgency of the objective, and the cost of delaying. Arrival does not force entry. Make a deliberate decision based on the company's actual state.

## 8 — Objective complete, plan changed

The original objective no longer determines the next move. Reassess the company's condition, hand, remaining resources, next objective, route legality, site path and Hazard exposure. The direct route from Redhorn Gate to Númeriador via Hollin, Rhudaur and Arthedain is five regions; do not declare it as a normal one-turn Region Movement.

## 9 — Map versus region card

Do not rely on the visual map alone. Region-card adjacency text is authoritative when it conflicts with the map. Find a legal route or choose a different destination.

## 10 — Route debrief

There is no single model route. A strong answer records the route actually used, what it exposed, a legal alternative, and a concrete reason to prefer that alternative. “It feels safer” is weaker than identifying a region type/name, creature key, destination reward, or next-position advantage.
