# Seized by Terror — Aftermath & Regrouping Lab: Model Answer

[Return to the exercise]({{ '/scenarios/seized-terror-aftermath-lab/' | relative_url }})

## 1. The roll against Óin

The result is 8 + mind 3 = **11**. Because 11 is less than 12, Seized by Terror succeeds.

Óin splits into a different company and immediately returns to the original company's site of origin: **Lórien**. He does not go to his home site or the nearest Haven chosen from a separate calculation.

The other four Dwarves continue the original Movement/Hazard Phase toward The Wind Throne.

## 2. River after Óin has split

No. Óin is no longer in the moving company at The Wind Throne, so he cannot tap there to cancel River. The remaining four Dwarves have no Ranger. The company must do nothing during its Site Phase and cannot enter The Wind Throne.

The return of Óin does not undo the original company's movement or give Óin a new Hazard phase.

## 3. The roll against Dori

The result is 10 + mind 1 = **11**. Seized by Terror succeeds and Dori returns to Lórien.

Óin remains with the moving company and can tap to cancel one River. If the Hazard player has played only one River, the company may proceed with its Site Phase. Dori's higher split probability does not automatically make him the best target when removing him leaves the only Ranger in place.

## 4. No second Hazard Limit

The split-off character receives no separate Movement/Hazard Phase and no new Hazard Limit of two. CoE Ruling Digest #52, Query 42, answers this point directly.

The original company's Hazard Limit was fixed at the beginning of its Movement/Hazard Phase. It remains the limit for the company that continues moving, even though that company is now smaller.

## 5. Palantír of Amon Sûl

It is **not** discarded immediately. CoE Ruling Digest #119, Query 2, states that a character successfully affected by Seized by Terror is no longer considered to be moving, so the Palantír is not discarded by this effect.

If the character attempts to move alone in a later Movement/Hazard Phase, the Palantír is discarded then. The ruling does not make the Palantír permanently safe from its normal movement restriction.

## 6. The target is a follower

The follower returns to the origin while its controlling character remains at the destination. Direct influence can control a follower only while the two characters are at the same site, so the follower is removed from that direct-influence control.

The follower is not immediately charged against general influence. At the next Organization Phase, the player must move it under general influence or under a character with sufficient direct influence at the same site. If it is not controlled by the end of that Organization Phase, it must be discarded.

This uses the ordinary follower rules in the consolidated rules and CRF 22; it is not a special additional effect printed on Seized by Terror.

## 7. The target is the controlling character

The controlling character's followers must remain stacked under that character. The controller and those followers therefore accompany the controller into the new company and return to the site of origin together.

This is different from targeting a follower, which separates that follower from the controller.

## 8. Promptings of Wisdom

The Ranger must tap **in response to the effect that would return him to the site of origin**, not merely in response to the initial play of Seized by Terror or before the split roll.

If Promptings cancels the return effect, the Ranger continues to the new site alone, without a separate Movement/Hazard Phase, and rejoins his company at the end of the turn's Movement/Hazard Phases. Promptings' protection follows the Ranger, so the original company is no longer protected by that effect.

If Seized by Terror targets a different character, Promptings remains with the company containing its Ranger and does not protect the split-off character.

This is the specific distinction in [CoE Digest #210 Q6](https://councilofelrond.org/forum/viewtopic.php?p=43305), posted 9 August 2025.

## 9. Regrouping

The two groups cannot join merely because they belong to the same player. They must be at the same site when the normal joining rules apply.

At the end of all Movement/Hazard Phases:

- Companies at the same non-Haven site must join, provided the resulting company is legal.
- Companies at the same Haven may join if the resource player chooses.
- Companies at different sites remain separate.

In the Lórien → The Wind Throne example, the main company is at The Wind Throne and the split group is back at Lórien. They cannot join that turn. A later movement that brings them to the same site can allow them to regroup under the normal rules.

A company returned to its site of origin cannot initiate actions during its Site Phase that turn.

## 10. Hazard Limit and further Hazards

The original moving company's Hazard Limit remains fixed. The Hazard player may continue playing legal Hazards against the company that is still moving, using any unspent portion of that original limit.

The returned character is not the current moving company, has no separate Hazard draw or Hazard Limit, and is not a second target for the original company's creature plays merely because it has split away. Check the exact target and keying rules for any other effect.

## Tactical summary

| Hero decision | Hazard consequence |
|---|---|
| Keep Óin untapped on a Shadow-land route | One River can be cancelled if he remains in the moving company. |
| Add Gildor as a second Ranger | One successful split cannot remove both Rangers; two Rivers still require both. |
| Let a follower carry an important item | If the follower is split, the item stays with that character, but the follower may lose direct-influence control. |
| Put an item on a high-mind controller | A successful split can send the controller and his stacked followers back to the origin; the item remains with its bearer. |
| Use Promptings of Wisdom on a Ranger | It can cancel the return effect when that Ranger is the target, but the protection follows him rather than the rest of the company. |

The central lesson is to track **where each character is after the split**, not merely whether the 2D6 roll succeeded. Then recalculate Ranger availability, follower control and the company's objective—without recalculating the Hazard Limit.
