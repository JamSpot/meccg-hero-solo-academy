# Hazard Combination Practice Lab — Answer Key

Attempt the [Hazard Combination Practice Lab]({{ '/scenarios/hazard-combo-lab/' | relative_url }}) first. These answers use the fixed 30-card Challenge Deck C-derived Hazard list, the five-Dwarf starting company, the Rivendell → Moria route, and the Academy's clearly labelled solo procedure. Check current card text and errata in the [Council of Elrond official card database](https://www.councilofelrond.org/cards/) and [official rules](https://councilofelrond.org/rules/).

## Drill 1 — Eight cards, five slots

The Rivendell → Moria site number for the Hazard side is 3, so the solo draw is 2 + (2 × 3) = **8 cards**. The five-character company has a base Hazard Limit of **5**. Play up to five limit-counting Hazards, selecting the most disadvantageous legal combination. Twilight is the exception: it never counts against the Hazard Limit, but it still needs an Environment card to cancel. There is no Environment target in the first-play baseline, so an unplayable Twilight cannot be used to fill a slot or create an attack.

## Drill 2 — The route makes the threat

The declared Region Movement path is Rhudaur → Hollin → Redhorn Gate: three consecutive Wilderness regions. Potential legal creatures include:

- **Cave Worm:** can use the named Redhorn Gate key.
- **Giant Spiders:** can use the double-Wilderness key.
- **Lesser Spiders, Wargs and Neeker-breekers:** can use a Wilderness key.
- **Watcher in the Water:** can use its double-Wilderness key and has a separate permission to be played at Moria.

A card is only a candidate. Confirm its exact printed key, that the named region or site is actually in the declared path, and that no other active condition prevents the play. Moria's site type does not turn its region into a Shadow-land.

## Drill 3 — Full of Froth and Rage plus Wake of War

Both support cards are Hazard plays and normally use one Hazard Limit slot each.

- **Full of Froth and Rage:** Spider and Animal attacks receive +2 prowess. It is discarded if a Spider or Animal attack is defeated.
- **Wake of War:** Wolf, Spider and Animal attacks receive +1 strike and +1 prowess. If Doors of Night is in play, Wolf attacks instead receive +2 strikes and +2 prowess. Doors of Night is not in the fixed baseline.
- When both effects apply to an ordinary variable-strike Spider/Animal attack, the combined modifier is **+3 prowess and +1 strike**.
- Watcher in the Water and Neeker-breekers specify one strike per character. The fixed strike count is not increased by a strike-count modifier, although the prowess modifier still applies.
- Full of Froth and Rage is temporary in practice: after each Spider or Animal attack, check whether that attack was defeated. If it was, discard the support event before resolving any later Spider/Animal attacks.

A sensible Hazard sequence is to resolve any creature that does not benefit from Full of Froth and Rage first (for example, Cave Worm), then establish the support and use it on the most threatening legal Spider/Animal attack. Do not assume the support will survive a defeated attack. The best order still depends on the company, its remaining avoidance cards and which attacks are actually legal.

## Drill 4 — A powerful creature with an invalid key

It cannot be played. Creature keying is an active condition; an unused Hazard Limit slot does not waive it. Return to the remaining legal cards and choose the most damaging line from those.

## Drill 5 — Moria is a Shadow-hold, not a Ruins & Lairs site

Moria is a **Shadow-hold** site. It is not a Ruins & Lairs site. Lesser Spiders or Neeker-breekers cannot use a Ruins & Lairs site key at Moria. On the Rivendell → Moria route, they can instead use their Wilderness region key because the path contains three Wilderness regions. Watcher in the Water has its own explicit Moria permission.

## Drill 6 — Watcher at Moria

Watcher in the Water is a legal candidate on this journey by its double-Wilderness key and by its explicit Moria site permission. Its attack makes each character face one strike at 8 prowess. It is one creature card and one Hazard Limit play, not one play per strike. Compare it with the other legal attacks and support cards before choosing it.

## Drill 7 — Disruption versus creature attack

- **Despair of the Heart:** a Corruption permanent-event. It can target a non-Wizard, non-Hobbit character, gives that character 2 corruption points, and makes the target check corruption each time a character in that target's company becomes wounded. The five starting Dwarves are eligible; Gandalf is not. This can turn even a wound to a different Dwarf into a corruption problem for the target.
- **River:** playable on the new site. A company that moved there this turn must do nothing during its Site Phase unless a Ranger taps to cancel the effect. Óin is a Ranger, so an untapped Óin can prevent the shutdown, but doing so costs his tap. If he is already tapped or cannot act, the company may lose its Site Phase actions. If the company declines to enter the site anyway, River may have no practical effect that turn.
- **Seized by Terror:** requires a non-Wizard character moving through a Shadow-land or Dark-domain region. The three-region Rivendell → Moria route contains only Wilderness, so it is not legal there. A Shadow-hold destination is a site type, not a Shadow-land region.

All three events count against the Hazard Limit when played in the Movement/Hazard Phase, except where a card has an explicit exception (Twilight).

## Drill 15 — Three Despairs and one wound

- **Three Despair cards can be played on three different Dwarves in the same Movement/Hazard Phase**, if all three are drawn and the Hazard Limit permits it. Despair is a Corruption card; the official rule limits this to one corruption card per character per turn. The card also says it cannot be duplicated on a given character. Neither restriction is a one-per-company cap.
- Each target receives 2 corruption points. Every time any character in that target's company becomes wounded, that target makes a corruption check. With three different targets, one wound can therefore trigger **three separate checks**. A wound to Gandalf also triggers the checks if he is in the same company, even though Gandalf himself cannot be targeted by Despair. With Gandalf joined, the company has six characters and the base Hazard Limit rises from five to six. Gandalf may tap to give +1 to a Dwarf's corruption check, but his personal +1 modifier applies only to his own checks; it does not automatically modify the Dwarf's roll.
- **Kíli and Óin are particularly vulnerable targets:** both have -1 to all their corruption checks. If either is carrying Book of Mazarbul, the Book adds 2 corruption points to Despair's 2, creating a 4-CP target with the -1 check modifier. This is a high-risk line, not an automatic elimination; the actual check must still be rolled.
- A strong five-slot line is **Despair ×3 + Watcher in the Water + Full of Froth and Rage**. Watcher is an Animal attack and each character faces one strike; Full of Froth adds +2 prowess. If any character is wounded, all three Despair targets check. The fixed one-strike-per-character wording means Wake of War's +1 strike does not add extra strikes to Watcher, although its +1 prowess would still apply. For the five-Dwarf company, playing both support events would take six Hazard slots and exceed its five-card limit. If Gandalf joins, the company has six characters and the limit rises to six, so the full line fits; Wake adds +1 prowess to Watcher but still adds no extra strikes.
- **Alternative lines are state-dependent.** Giant Spiders plus Full of Froth gives two high-prowess Spider strikes and can be preferable if the company has a vulnerable defender; River can replace an attack if denying the Site Phase is more valuable. Do not call one line universally best.
- The Hero player can tap other characters in the company to add +1 to a corruption check, but each tap is a limited resource. The target may try to remove Despair during Organization by tapping and rolling greater than 4; if already tapped, consult the rules' -3 alternative for attempting the removal without tapping again.

## Drill 8 — Company does not move

The company still has a Movement/Hazard Phase, but it has no new site path. The current site is treated as both origin and new site for relevant checks. A creature may still be keyed to the current site's type or name if its card text permits it. The standard rules' card-draw step applies when the company moves; the printed solo multiplier does not clearly settle every stationary-company draw case. Use a scenario-specific instruction rather than inventing a universal draw number.

## Drill 9 — Same destination, different route

Compare both the region types and names in each declared path, not just the destination. A Region Movement route reveals every region name; Starter Movement usually reveals only the origin and destination names, if it is legal. A route exposing three Wilderness regions can enable double-Wilderness creatures; a route through a Shadow-land or Dark-domain could make Seized by Terror legal. The Hero player should weigh Hazard exposure against the value of the destination and the next position. The Hazard player should select the route's strongest legal keys, not assume the route with the most regions is automatically best.

## Drill 10 — Roadblock and automatic attack

The exact cards must be named before a ruling can be made. In general, a Hazard may target a company's new site during the Movement/Hazard Phase. A site automatic attack can be modified at that time even if the company later chooses not to enter; the Hazard play still counts against the Hazard Limit. If the company declines to enter, it does not face that automatic attack during the Site Phase. Check each card's target, timing and duration rather than inferring a combination from its theme.

## Drill 11 — Three-game evidence

Do not change the 30-card list during the baseline. Record the actual draw, route, legal cards, plays, taps, wounds, site-entry decision and whether the Hazard changed the expedition. A card not drawn is **not tested**; a card drawn but illegal is a route/keying observation; a legal card with no impact is evidence about its actual target or timing. Only promote a proposed swap after the same baseline has been played three times and the observed weakness is specific.

## Drill 12 — Twilight with no Environment target

Twilight does not count against the Hazard Limit, but its effect still requires an Environment card in play or declared earlier in the same chain of effects. It cannot cancel Fellowship, Full of Froth and Rage, Wake of War or another non-Environment event. The active Hero main deck and the fixed Hazard deck do not supply an Environment target in Game 1; Gates of Morning is in the sideboard and is not being used. Under the Academy's selected supplementary solo procedure, an unplayable drawn Twilight is discarded at the end of that company's Movement/Hazard Phase. The no-limit ruling does not turn it into a free attack.

## Drill 13 — Arouse Minions (candidate only)

Arouse Minions increases the prowess of one automatic attack at a Shadow-hold or Dark-hold site by +3 until the end of the turn. Moria and Goblin-gate are both Shadow-holds, so the card is legal at either site. It does not add strikes. It counts against the Hazard Limit. It can be played during the Movement/Hazard Phase on the new site's automatic attack even if the Hero company later declines to enter; if the company does not enter, that attack is not faced during the Site Phase. It remains a proposed replacement for one Twilight, not part of the locked baseline.

## Drill 14 — Brigands (candidate only)

Brigands is a Men creature with two strikes at 8 prowess, keyed to Border-lands or Wilderness. Each of the three Academy route legs contains Wilderness, so it is a legal candidate on those routes. If a strike wounds a character, the defending player chooses one item for the company to discard. The card is one Hazard play against the limit, regardless of its two strikes. It is a proposed replacement for one Seized by Terror, not part of the active 30-card list.

## Final Hazard-side checklist

- [ ] I drew the correct solo number and kept it separate from the Hazard Limit.
- [ ] I checked every creature's region/site key.
- [ ] I checked each event's target and timing.
- [ ] I counted Twilight correctly: no Hazard Limit cost, but still needs an Environment target.
- [ ] I checked whether Full of Froth and Rage survived each Spider/Animal attack.
- [ ] I considered Despair of the Heart before selecting a wound-producing attack.
- [ ] I considered River's cost against the Ranger and the Hero's planned Site Phase.
- [ ] I did not play an illegal card just to use a slot.
- [ ] I selected the most disadvantageous legal sequence for the actual company.
