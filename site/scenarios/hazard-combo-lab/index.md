# Hazard Combination Practice Lab

Use this workbook with the physical Dwarven Quest / Pallando Challenge Deck C Hazard list. The aim is to learn how to identify the strongest **legal** combination, not to invent interactions from card names. Keep the 30-card list unchanged during the baseline tests.

Before each exercise, read the printed text of every relevant card and check the [official rules](https://councilofelrond.org/rules/). If the answer depends on wording you have not checked, mark it **unverified** rather than guessing. Complete the [Site-Keying Workshop]({{ '/scenarios/site-keying-workshop/' | relative_url }}) first if lowercase region types and uppercase site types are still easy to confuse. Complete the drills before opening the [detailed answer key]({{ '/scenarios/hazard-combo-lab/answer-key/' | relative_url }}).

## Drill 1 — Eight cards, five slots

A five-character company moves from Rivendell to Moria. Under the Academy's selected solo draw procedure, eight Hazard cards are drawn. Four cards appear legal.

**Task:** How many Hazards may be played, and should a useful legal play be skipped simply because it looks less dramatic?

**Model principle:** the solo procedure asks the Hazard player to play as many of the drawn Hazards as possible within the Hazard Limit, choosing the most disadvantageous legal combination. The eight-card draw is not eight plays; the base Hazard Limit for this five-character company is five. Never make an illegal card legal just to fill a slot.

## Drill 2 — The route makes the threat

The company declares Region Movement on the teaching route **Rhudaur → Hollin → Redhorn Gate**.

**Task:** Count the regions and identify the region types. Check the actual Challenge Deck C creature cards against the route and destination.

**Model principle:** all three regions are Wilderness, and the destination is Moria. Cave Worm can be keyed to the named region Redhorn Gate; Giant Spiders can use the double-Wilderness key; Lesser Spiders, Wargs and Neeker-breekers have Wilderness among their keys; Watcher in the Water may also be played at Moria. These are potential legal candidates, not automatic plays: verify the printed card, the declared path, timing and remaining Hazard Limit.

## Drill 3 — Full of Froth and Rage plus Wake of War

Both named support cards are in the drawn Hazard hand, along with a creature.

**Task:** Before playing anything, check whether either support card is legal now, what it affects, its timing, and whether both can legally affect the same attack or phase. Count Hazard Limit use.

**Model principle:** Full of Froth and Rage gives Spider and Animal attacks +2 prowess; Wake of War gives Wolf, Spider and Animal attacks +1 strike and +1 prowess (or Wolves +2 strikes and +2 prowess if Doors of Night is active). When both effects apply to a fixed-strike Spider/Animal attack, the combined modifier is +3 prowess and +1 strike. Full of Froth and Rage does not boost Wolves. Watcher in the Water and Neeker-breekers are special cases: their text says each character faces one strike, so the ruling digest says fixed strike-increase effects do not add extra strikes to those attacks. The Neeker-breekers erratum also says the card does not affect Wizards. **Full of Froth and Rage is discarded if a Spider or Animal attack is defeated**; check whether it survives after every such attack before applying its bonus to a later one. Check the printed cards and ruling before resolving the example.

**Limit reminder:** Twilight is the fixed list's special exception. It may be played at any point during either player's turn and does not count against the Hazard Limit, but it must have a legal Environment card to cancel. It is not a general extra attack.

## Drill 4 — A powerful creature with an invalid key

A creature looks like the strongest play in the hand, but the declared path or destination does not satisfy its printed key.

**Task:** Can you play it anyway because the Hazard Limit has unused space?

**Model principle:** no. An unused Hazard Limit slot does not override creature keying. Choose among the remaining legal cards.

## Drill 5 — Moria is a Shadow-hold, not a Ruins & Lairs site

The Moria site card has site type **Shadow-hold [{S}]**. It is not a Ruins & Lairs [{R}] site.

**Task:** Lesser Spiders and Neeker-breekers both include a Ruins & Lairs site key. Can you use that key at Moria? If they are legal on the Rhudaur → Hollin → Redhorn Gate route, identify the actual key that makes them legal.

**Model principle:** not via the site type. Moria is {S}, not {R}; the cards are candidates here through the route's Wilderness [{w}] region key. Watcher in the Water is different because its own text explicitly permits play at Moria.

## Drill 6 — Watcher at Moria

The company is moving to Moria and Watcher in the Water is in the drawn hand.

**Task:** Use the actual card text and current rules to determine whether it is keyed legally to this company or site in this situation. Then compare it with any other legal play.

**Model principle:** do not decide from the card's name or theme. Verify the exact key, timing, target, and any other conditions. If legal, compare the actual consequence with the other available plays.

## Drill 7 — Disruption versus creature attack

Despair of the Heart, River, and Seized by Terror appear in a hand with a creature.

**Task:** For each event, identify its legal target, timing, and conditions from the card text. Does it threaten the Hero objective, and does it use Hazard Limit?

**Model principle:** these are not interchangeable cards. Evaluate each according to its own text and the current company state. Do not claim a combo until you have identified a real interaction or a clear sequence of separate pressures.

## Drill 8 — Company does not move

The company remains at its current site for the turn.

**Task:** Does it still have a Movement/Hazard Phase? What kind of creatures, if any, can be keyed to its situation?

**Model principle:** a company that does not move still has a Movement/Hazard Phase, but there is no newly declared region site path. Use the current site and official rules to determine which plays are legal.

## Drill 9 — Same destination, different route

Two legal routes reach the same destination. Route A exposes a region type that makes a creature legal; Route B provides a better next position but has a different exposure.

**Task:** Which route is better for the Hero company? Which is better for the Hazard player?

**Model principle:** there is no universal answer. Compare objective value, route legality, creature keying, company condition, and the position after arrival. A route can be legal yet strategically poor.

## Drill 10 — Roadblock and automatic attack

A roadblock effect and an automatic-attack modifier are both available.

**Task:** Identify the exact cards, timing window, target, and duration. Can the modifier legally affect this automatic attack? Does the combination use Hazard Limit?

**Model principle:** do not assume a card can be played on-guard or at any point in the phase. Confirm both texts and the relevant rule before treating this as a combo.

## Drill 11 — Three-game evidence

Run the same Hero route three times with the same 30-card Hazard list. Do not change quantities mid-test.

After the third game, distinguish:
- **Not seen:** the card did not appear; no judgement yet.
- **Seen but illegal:** check whether the route made it unusable.
- **Legal but low impact:** record the actual board state.
- **Useful interaction confirmed:** cite the card wording or ruling.
- **Needs further research:** do not promote it to a recommended combo yet.

## Drill 12 — Twilight with no Environment target

You draw **Twilight** during the first-play baseline. No Environment card is in play or declared earlier in the current chain of effects. The active 36-card Hero main deck has no Environment Resource; Gates of Morning is in the sideboard and is not being used in this baseline.

**Task:** Can Twilight be played just because it does not count against the Hazard Limit? Can it be held for a later company or phase?

**Model principle:** no. Twilight needs an Environment card to cancel. Its no-limit status does not remove the target requirement. Under the Academy's supplementary solo procedure, discard it with the other unplayed drawn Hazards at the end of the company's Movement/Hazard Phase. It becomes useful if a later campaign introduces an Environment target, such as Gates of Morning.

## Drill 13 — Arouse Minions (candidate card, not in the active list)

For a future controlled test only, imagine one **Arouse Minions** has replaced one Twilight. The company moves to Moria, whose Hero site card is a Shadow-hold.

**Task:** Is Arouse Minions legal on Moria? What does it modify, and what happens if the Hero company declines to enter the site?

**Model principle:** it is a Hazard short-event playable on a Shadow-hold or Dark-hold. It increases one automatic attack at the target site by +3 prowess for the turn; it does not add strikes. It is also legal at Goblin-gate, because Goblin-gate is a Shadow-hold like Moria. It counts against the Hazard Limit. If the company later declines to enter Moria, the site automatic attack is not faced that turn, so the event may have produced no practical effect. This card is a non-Dragon candidate for later tuning, not part of the locked 30-card baseline. Do not add it until the first three baseline games are recorded.

## Drill 14 — Brigands (candidate card, not in the active list)

For a future controlled test only, imagine one **Brigands** has replaced one Seized by Terror. The Hero company is moving along one of the Academy's Wilderness routes and carries Book of Mazarbûl plus several other items.

**Task:** Check the creature's region key. How many strikes does it make? If one strike wounds a character, who chooses the item discarded? Does the card count as one or two Hazard plays against the Hazard Limit?

**Model principle:** Brigands is a Men creature with two strikes at 8 prowess, playable keyed to Border-lands or Wilderness. It is legal on each of the three opening Wilderness routes. If any strike wounds a character, the company immediately discards one item chosen by the defender (the Hero player). The creature is one Hazard card and therefore one Hazard play against the limit; its two strikes do not count as two separate Hazard cards. This is a non-Dragon way to put direct pressure on an item-rich Hero company. It is a proposed v1.1 replacement for one Seized by Terror, not part of the active 30-card baseline.

## Drill 15 — Three Despairs and one wound

The five-Dwarf company moves to Moria. The constructed eight-card Hazard hand contains **Despair of the Heart ×3, Watcher in the Water, Full of Froth and Rage, Wake of War, Giant Spiders and River**. Assume the Dwarves are eligible targets and no Despair is already on them.

**Task:** Can all three Despairs be played in this one Movement/Hazard Phase, and how must each be timed in relation to a new chain of effects? May two target the same Dwarf? If Watcher wounds one or more characters, how many corruption checks can one wound trigger? Compare the line of three Despairs + Watcher + Full of Froth with an alternative that uses Giant Spiders or River. Keep the five-Dwarf Hazard Limit in view.

Then repeat with Dori, Kíli or Óin as both the Despair target and Book bearer, and with Gandalf joined to the company carrying the Book instead. None of the five starting Dwarves is a Sage, so their Book bearer does not gain its Sage-only hand-size ability. Identify who may be targeted, what the Book adds, and how the extra character changes the Hazard Limit and the available corruption-check support.

**Model principle:** the restriction is per character, not per company. Three Despairs can target three different eligible Dwarves, but cannot be stacked on one Dwarf. Each target checks whenever any character in the company becomes wounded. The Hazard side can therefore turn one wound into several checks; it must still spend one Hazard Limit slot per Despair and choose the strongest legal attack line.
