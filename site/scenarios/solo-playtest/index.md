# Solo Playtest Protocol — Hero and Hazard Decks

This protocol turns the Academy's two 30-card teaching decks into a repeatable learning exercise. It is designed for genuine physical cards and the Academy's explicitly labelled solo teaching hybrid. It does not claim that the lists are tournament-optimised or that the hybrid is an official multiplayer ruleset.

## Purpose

After three short sessions, aim to answer:
1. Can I plan a legal route without automatically returning to a Haven after every expedition?
2. Do I understand how the declared site path creates creature-Hazard opportunities?
3. Can I choose strong legal Hazard plays without exceeding the Hazard Limit?
4. Does the Hero deck give me useful choices before, during and after an expedition?
5. When is recovery better than forcing an objective with a weakened company?
6. Which decision caused a problem, rather than which card happened to be present?

## Before you start

Keep separate: the 30-card Hero Resource deck, the 30-card Hazard deck, Location deck, character pool, Hero and Hazard discard piles, and any sideboard. Keep the sideboard out of the beginner test unless the selected rules explicitly call for it.

Read [Which Solo Rules Are We Using?]({{ '/reference/solo-ruleset/' | relative_url }}) first. The Academy combines normal MECCG rules for movement, site paths, card legality and Hazard Limits with a separately identified solitaire draw procedure. Do not silently import individual rules from another solo system.

## Test A — Baseline expedition

**Mission:** start at Rivendell and travel to Moria to attempt to acquire Book of Mazarbul. Use the Hero and Hazard lists unchanged.

Before declaring movement, fill in:

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Current site and region | |
| Objective | Acquire Book of Mazarbul |
| Destination and destination region | |
| Movement type | |
| Proposed region sequence | |
| Region count, including both endpoints | |
| Every adjacency checked on the region cards? | Yes / No |
| Any repeated region? | Yes / No |
| Plan if the company arrives weakened | |

The teaching Region Movement route is **Rhudaur → Hollin → Redhorn Gate**. Count all three regions and verify adjacency using the physical region cards. Choose the destination and declare movement during Organization. Declare the Region Movement site path later, during the Movement/Hazard Phase after the new site is revealed.

### Hazard decision record

For each drawn Hazard, classify it before choosing plays:

- Legal now
- Illegal for this company, site path or destination
- Legal only if another condition is met
- Unsure — check the printed text or rules

Then choose the strongest legal combination under the Academy's selected solo procedure, respecting the company's Hazard Limit and each card's wording. Do not treat the number drawn as the number that may be played.

For the standard five-character teaching company travelling to Moria, the Academy procedure draws **8** cards (2 + twice Moria's normal Hazard draw number of 3); the normal base Hazard Limit is **5**. These numbers answer different questions.

### Arrival is not site entry

After the Movement/Hazard Phase, record the company's actual condition before deciding whether to enter:

| Check | Record |
|---|---|
| Characters still in the company | |
| Wounds / eliminated characters | |
| Characters tapped | |
| Key defensive or movement cards spent | |
| Cards remaining in hand | |
| Site effects and automatic attacks checked? | Yes / No |
| Decision | Enter / Recover / Other legal option |
| Reason | |

Do not mark a route as good merely because the company survived. Ask whether it arrived able to accomplish the objective and whether the risk was worth the expected reward.

## Test B — Compare two legal routes

On a fresh setup, compare two movement options that your physical region cards and site cards actually permit. Do not invent an adjacency to make the exercise work.

Score each option from 0 to 2: legality confidence, Hazard exposure, objective value, recovery/next position, and company readiness. Use 0 for poor or uncertain, 1 for mixed, and 2 for strong. Record a total out of 10.

| Route option | Legality /2 | Exposure /2 | Objective /2 | Next position /2 | Readiness /2 | Total /10 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Route A | | | | | | |
| Route B | | | | | | |

This score is a thinking aid, not a game rule. An illegal route is never rescued by a good score. Write down which fact would make you change your choice.

## Test C — The recovery decision

Practise a turn in which the company reaches a destination with its best fighter tapped or wounded and an important avoidance resource already spent. This is a decision drill; you do not need to pretend that a particular random draw occurred.

Before entering the site, answer:
1. What objective remains worth pursuing?
2. What site effects or automatic attacks must be resolved?
3. Which characters can still perform the necessary actions?
4. What is the cost of failing the attempt?
5. Is there a legal recovery, repositioning or return option?
6. What new information would justify pressing on?

**Model principle:** reaching the destination does not oblige the company to enter or attempt the objective regardless of condition. Make the decision using the actual rules and current board state.

## Hero deck audit

After each game, classify each Hero resource card that was drawn or considered:

- **Useful now:** made a clear contribution to the current plan.
- **Useful later:** retained for a credible future need.
- **Situational:** useful only when a particular condition occurs.
- **Dead in hand:** no legal or realistic use arose during this test.
- **Not seen:** cannot judge from this game.

A card that was not drawn is not a dead card. A situational card is not automatically bad. Record the situation in which you expected it to help and whether that situation actually occurred.

After three games, look for patterns: repeated unusable cards, missing answers to a problem that genuinely occurred, or resources competing for the same narrow opportunity. Only then propose a change, and change one small group at a time so the next test can tell you what changed.

## Hazard deck audit

For each Movement/Hazard Phase, record:

| Measure | Result |
|---|---|
| Cards drawn under the chosen solo procedure | |
| Cards with a legal play available | |
| Cards actually played | |
| Hazard Limit reached? | Yes / No |
| Any card held back because its requirement was not met? | |
| Did the route make a creature legal by region type or name? | |
| Did the combination materially change the expedition? | |
| Was any play uncertain and later checked? | |

The aim is not to make the Hazard side win every encounter. Apply the strongest legal pressure consistently so Hero decisions can be compared from one test to the next. If a card is uncertain, check its printed wording and the rules rather than improvising.

## Five practice scenarios

**1 — Eight drawn, only three legal.** A five-character company moves to Moria. Eight cards are drawn under the solo procedure, but only three currently have legal plays.  
**Model approach:** play only legal cards, up to the Hazard Limit, following the selected procedure's instruction to play as many drawn Hazards as possible. Do not turn an illegal card into a legal one to fill a slot.

**2 — Two routes, same destination.** Both routes are legal, but one exposes more named regions while the other leaves a better next position.  
**Model approach:** compare creature keying, the objective, company condition and next position. The site path matters for Hazard keying; it does not itself move the company.

**3 — Powerful card, invalid key.** A Hazard looks devastating, but its printed condition or key cannot be met.  
**Model approach:** it is not a legal option. Choose from the remaining legal plays and check any sequencing requirements on the actual cards.

**4 — Book acquired, company weakened.** The company has acquired Book of Mazarbul but ends the expedition with characters tapped and a fighter wounded.  
**Model approach:** reassess the next objective, recovery options and route before declaring another move. Do not continue merely because the next destination is reachable.

**5 — Same resource repeatedly unused.** A card remains in hand through several games.  
**Model approach:** record whether a legal opportunity arose, whether the card was needed for a future plan, and whether another card repeatedly displaced it. Consider changing it only after repeated evidence.

## Ready-to-review checklist

- [ ] I explain the solo draw separately from the normal Hazard Limit.
- [ ] I check route legality before movement is declared.
- [ ] I distinguish the movement route from the declared site path.
- [ ] I check each Hazard's printed key, target and conditions.
- [ ] I make a separate decision about entering the site.
- [ ] I can explain why each Hero resource is in the teaching list.
- [ ] I have completed three logs before proposing deck changes.
- [ ] Any unresolved ruling is marked for verification, not guessed.
