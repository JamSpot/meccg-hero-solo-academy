# Route Risk Workshop — choose the expedition, not just the path

This workshop teaches you to compare routes before moving. It uses the genuine physical Dwarven Quest / Pallando Challenge Deck C teaching setup; no proxies or additional cards are needed.

Read the [Free / Player-Directed Movement guide]({{ '/reference/free-movement/' | relative_url }}) first. Keep the [Hero deck]({{ '/decks/hero-deck/' | relative_url }}) and [Hazard deck]({{ '/decks/hazard-deck/' | relative_url }}) nearby, and use the [Expedition Planner]({{ '/routes/expedition-planner/' | relative_url }}) to record your final choice.

## The rule: legality is a gate, not a score

Do not award a route points for being fast if it is illegal. Check these first:

1. Is the chosen movement type allowed between these sites?
2. Does the route include the origin and destination regions?
3. Are all adjacent region pairs legal according to the physical Region cards?
4. Are there four or fewer regions for normal Region Movement?
5. Is any region repeated?
6. Can you explain which site path will be declared and which Hazards it may enable?

Only routes that pass all relevant legality checks should be compared strategically.

## The Captain's route scorecard

Use this simple 0–2 score for **each legal route**. It is a learning aid, not an official game rule.

| Factor | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mission value | Does not advance the mission | Indirect or optional value | Directly advances the stated mission |
| Hazard exposure | Several serious legal threats look relevant | Some relevant threats | Fewer or less consequential threats appear relevant |
| Arrival condition | Likely to leave little capacity for the objective | Uncertain | Preserves the company's ability to act |
| Next position | Poor or costly follow-up | Acceptable | Opens a useful, legal next expedition |
| Fallback | No credible plan if weakened | Limited fallback | Clear recovery or alternate objective |

Before scoring Hazard exposure, inspect the actual cards in the Hazard hand and their printed keying. Do not treat a creature as legal merely because its theme fits the journey. Do not treat a low exposure score as proof that a route is safe.

**Use the score to explain your reasoning, not to replace it.** A single decisive rule or card condition can outweigh a total.

## Drill 1 — The first expedition

**State:** The five-character starting company is at Rivendell. The mission is to acquire Book of Mazarbul at Moria.

**Proposed Region Movement route:** Rhudaur → Hollin → Redhorn Gate.

Write down:

- the region count, including both endpoints;
- the adjacency checks you made using the physical Region cards;
- the region types and names exposed by the declared site path;
- the main creature keys you would check in the drawn Hazard hand;
- the solo Hazard draw and the base Hazard Limit as two separate numbers;
- the decision you will make if the company arrives wounded or tapped.

Do not assume that reaching Moria obliges you to attempt the objective immediately.

## Drill 2 — The tempting direct journey

**State:** Book of Mazarbul has been acquired. You are considering Blue Mountain Dwarf-hold as the next objective.

**Proposed route:** Redhorn Gate → Hollin → Rhudaur → Arthedain → Númeriador.

Is this a legal single normal Region Movement? If not, split it into legal legs using the Academy's verified teaching example. Check adjacency on the physical Region cards before moving.

Then explain what the intermediate stop offers beyond simply making the journey legal.

## Drill 3 — The repeated region

A player proposes this abstract route:

**A → B → C → B → D**

Assume the origin, destination and each region name are otherwise appropriate.

- Is the route legal as normal Region Movement?
- Why does removing the repeated region matter?
- What should you do if the only route you have written down repeats a region?

Do not invent a connection to make the route work. Return to the physical Region cards and find a legal alternative or change the destination.

## Drill 4 — The Hazard hand changes the route decision

Two routes to the same objective have already passed the movement-legality checks.

- **Route A** exposes three Wilderness regions and names Redhorn Gate.
- **Route B** has a different legal site path and leaves the company better positioned for the next objective.
- Your drawn Hazard hand contains a creature whose printed key may match Route A.

Your tasks:

1. Check the creature's exact key, target and any other conditions.
2. Decide whether it is actually legal on Route A.
3. Compare the current objective, potential Hazard pressure, likely arrival condition and next position for both routes.
4. Explain why the Hazard player may prefer a different route from the Hero player.
5. Record what evidence would make you reverse your choice.

Route A is a teaching comparison based on the Academy's Rivendell–Moria path; Route B is deliberately abstract. Do not assume an unverified real-world alternate route is legal.

## Drill 5 — Eight cards, five Hazard slots

The five-character company moves to Moria. Under the Academy's selected solo procedure, eight Hazard cards are drawn.

Explain:

- what the draw of eight means;
- why the base Hazard Limit is five;
- why a card that is illegal on the declared site path is not an option;
- why you should not automatically play the first five cards;
- how you will compare legal creatures, events and support effects.

Use the [Hazard Combination Lab]({{ '/decks/hazard-combos/' | relative_url }}) to check interactions. If the exact printed wording or timing is unclear, mark the interaction unverified and consult the [official rules and rulings]({{ '/reference/solo-ruleset/' | relative_url }}).

## Drill 6 — The weakened arrival

The company reaches the objective site. Its best fighter is wounded, two characters are tapped and the most useful avoidance resource has already been spent.

Before entering, inspect:

- the site's printed requirements and automatic attack;
- which characters can still contribute;
- the remaining legal defensive or avoidance resources;
- the value and urgency of the objective;
- the cost of delaying, recovering or changing the next destination.

Write two possible decisions and the facts that would support each. “Always enter” and “always retreat” are not sufficient answers.

## Drill 7 — Travel between non-Haven sites

After acquiring Book of Mazarbul, you are considering a second objective: Gollum at Goblin-gate. The company is still at Moria.

**Proposed route:** Redhorn Gate → Hollin → Rhudaur → High Pass.

Before you decide, answer:

1. Is Goblin-gate in High Pass, and have you confirmed the site and region cards?
2. How many regions are in the sequence when you count both endpoints?
3. Have you checked every adjacency and confirmed there are no repeated regions?
4. Does this use the normal four-region Region Movement limit?
5. What does the four-Wilderness site path expose to the Hazard player?
6. What automatic attack must be faced if you enter Goblin-gate?
7. What would make a Haven stop worth the extra movement, even though it is not mandatory?

**Do not assume the direct route is best just because it is legal.** Compare the value of the Gollum objective with the Hazard exposure, the Goblin-gate automatic attack and the company's actual wounds, taps and remaining defensive resources.

Reference: the historical Challenge Deck C player aid lists Gollum for Goblin-gate; the full site list identifies Goblin-gate as a Shadow-hold in High Pass with an Orc automatic attack of three strikes at 6 prowess. Verify the physical cards and current rules before play.

## Three-game route evidence log

Keep both teaching lists unchanged for these three tests.

| Game | Mission and route | Legal Hazards actually available | Company condition on arrival | Did the route help the next objective? | One change to test |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | | | | | |
| 2 | | | | | |
| 3 | | | | | |

Classify each apparent Hazard failure as one of these:

- **Not drawn** — no conclusion about legality.
- **Drawn but illegal** — record which key or condition failed.
- **Legal but low impact** — record the actual company state.
- **High impact** — record the rule, card text and effect on the mission.
- **Unverified** — stop and research rather than assuming.
