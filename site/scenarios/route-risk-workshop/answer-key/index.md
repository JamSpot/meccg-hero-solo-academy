# Route Risk Workshop — Model Answers

These answers show the reasoning to check, not a single compulsory tactical choice. Card wording, the physical Region cards, the current company state and the selected solo procedure still govern the decision.

## Drill 1 — The first expedition

Rhudaur → Hollin → Redhorn Gate contains **three regions**, including origin and destination. It is within the normal four-region maximum, has no repeated region, and is the Academy's teaching route from Rivendell to Moria. Check every adjacency against the physical Region cards before declaring movement.

The path contains three Wilderness regions and names Redhorn Gate. That can make a double-Wilderness creature or a creature keyed to Redhorn Gate a candidate, but only when its exact printed key and all other conditions are met.

Under the Academy's selected solo draw procedure, Moria's normal Hazard draw number of 3 gives **2 + (2 × 3) = 8 Hazard cards** for this non-Haven destination. The five-character company's normal base Hazard Limit is **5**. Eight is the number drawn under this solo variant; five is the normal number of Hazard plays that count against the limit for this company and phase, subject to any applicable rule or effect.

On arrival, reassess wounds, tapped characters, resources already spent, the site's automatic attack and the value of the objective. A legal route does not force an immediate site attempt.

## Drill 2 — The tempting direct journey

Redhorn Gate → Hollin → Rhudaur → Arthedain → Númeriador has **five regions**. It exceeds the normal four-region limit, so it is not legal as one normal Region Movement.

The Academy's teaching split is:

1. **Moria → Rivendell:** Redhorn Gate → Hollin → Rhudaur.
2. **Rivendell → Blue Mountain Dwarf-hold:** Rhudaur → Arthedain → Númeriador.

Check adjacency and destination availability before declaring each leg. Rivendell is a convenient Haven stop in this example; it is not a universal requirement to return to a Haven. An intermediate stop can create a chance to recover, manage the hand, reassess the objective and prepare for the next leg.

## Drill 3 — The repeated region

A → B → C → B → D is not legal as normal Region Movement because **B repeats**. The route must not revisit a region within the same declared sequence. Find another legal sequence using the Region cards or choose a different destination; do not make up an adjacency.

## Drill 4 — The Hazard hand changes the route decision

The first question is not “which route looks safer?” It is “which threats are actually legal on each route?”

For Route A, inspect the exact creature card and check its key, any required number of region types, named-region or site key, target and other conditions. Three Wilderness regions can satisfy a two-Wilderness requirement if the card's wording and the rest of the conditions allow it. A matching theme or region type alone is not enough.

Then compare the complete expedition. Route A may offer direct progress but make a relevant creature legal; Route B may improve the next position but change the immediate exposure. The Hero player values mission progress and survival; the Hazard player evaluates the strongest legal disruption. The scorecard is a way to expose the trade-off, not a game rule or a guaranteed numerical prediction.

Because Route B is abstract, no specific adjacency or creature legality can be concluded for it without an actual proposed route and the relevant physical cards.

## Drill 5 — Eight cards, five Hazard slots

Eight cards are drawn because of the Academy's selected solo procedure; that does not override the normal Hazard Limit. A five-character company has a base limit of five under the normal rule. Each candidate still has to be legal at declaration and resolution.

Do not automatically play the first five cards. Check keying, target, timing, conditions and interactions; then compare the strongest legal combination within the remaining limit. If fewer than five candidates are legal, do not fill a slot with an illegal card. Do not assume two support effects stack unless their text and the relevant ruling allow it.

## Drill 6 — The weakened arrival

There is no automatic answer. A sound response checks the site's printed requirements and automatic attack, the remaining company's ability to survive, available resources, objective urgency, and the consequences of delaying. Attempting now may be justified if the objective is essential and the company can reasonably handle the risk. Recovery or a changed plan may be better if the likely cost would destroy the company's ability to complete the wider campaign.

State the evidence behind the decision and what new information would change it.

## Drill 7 — Travel between non-Haven sites

Goblin-gate is in **High Pass**. The proposed sequence is **Redhorn Gate → Hollin → Rhudaur → High Pass**. It contains four regions, including origin and destination. If each adjacency is confirmed on the physical Region cards, it has no repeated region and fits the normal four-region limit for Region Movement.

This is the Academy's concrete example of moving from one non-Haven site to another without returning to a Haven. The route is legal only if the region-card adjacency checks pass; never use the written example as a substitute for checking the actual components.

All four regions are Wilderness. Under Region Movement, their names are included in the declared site path, so check the exact keying of each drawn creature. Do not assume every Wilderness creature can be played: its printed key, target and other conditions still control.

Goblin-gate has an automatic attack of **Orcs — three strikes with 6 prowess**. This is resolved during the site encounter if the company enters. It is not an extra Hazard card and does not count as a Hazard play against the Hazard Limit.

The trip can save a Haven detour, but it does not provide the recovery opportunity a Haven might offer. If the company is weakened, a Haven stop or a different legal destination may be the better strategic choice. The Gollum objective is a reason to consider Goblin-gate, not an obligation to go there immediately.

## Three-game evidence log

The log is useful only if the setup remains comparable. Keep both 30-card teaching lists unchanged, record the actual route and drawn cards, and distinguish “not drawn” from “drawn but illegal” and “legal but low impact.” One game is not enough to conclude that a card is weak. If you propose a change, name the specific repeated problem, the replacement, the expected benefit and the next test that could disprove your theory.
