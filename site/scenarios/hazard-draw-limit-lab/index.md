# Hazard Draw & Limit Lab — calculate before you draw

This workbook teaches four numbers that are easy to confuse:

1. **Normal Hazard draw number** — the number printed in the dark Hazard-draw box on the relevant site card.
2. **Solo Hazard draw** — the number of cards the Academy's selected solitaire procedure tells the Hazard side to draw.
3. **Base Hazard Limit** — the number of limit-counting Hazard plays normally allowed against this company in this Movement/Hazard Phase.
4. **Modified Hazard Limit** — the limit after a card or rule changes it, such as Hundreds of Butterflies.

The draw tells you how many cards are available to consider. The limit tells you how many ordinary Hazard plays can count against the company. They are not the same number.

## The rules sequence

### Step 1 — Which site's normal Hazard draw number applies?

Use the normal movement rule first:

- Moving to a **non-Haven**: use the destination site's normal Hazard draw number.
- Moving to a **Haven**: use the site of origin's normal Hazard draw number.

Do not use the destination Haven's number just because that is where the company will arrive.

### Step 2 — Apply the Academy solo procedure

The selected solitaire procedure is:

**Solo Hazard draw = 2 + (2 × normal Hazard draw number)**

Examples:

- Normal draw 1 → 4 solo cards.
- Normal draw 2 → 6 solo cards.
- Normal draw 3 → 8 solo cards.

This is the established Hero/Wizard solitaire procedure used by the Academy. It is not the ordinary multiplayer draw rule.

### Step 3 — Calculate the company's base Hazard Limit

Under normal rules, the base Hazard Limit is the company's size or two, whichever is greater.

For company size:
- A normal character counts as 1.
- Each Hobbit or Orc scout counts as ½.
- Fractions are rounded up.

The base limit is set at the beginning of that company's Movement/Hazard Phase and stays fixed for the phase, even if a character is later eliminated. Card effects can still modify the limit.

### Step 4 — Apply relevant card effects

- **Hundreds of Butterflies**: when played on a moving character during the Movement/Hazard Phase, it untaps that character and increases the Hazard Limit against the company by one. It does not change the number of cards already drawn by the solo formula.
- **Twilight**: does not count against the Hazard Limit. It can cancel a legal Environment target, but it is not a generic extra Hazard play; check its timing and target.
- **Sideboard access**: in normal rules, if the Hazard player accessed the sideboard during that turn's Untap Phase, the base Hazard Limit is halved, rounded up. The Academy's opening teaching game does not access the Hazard sideboard, so this is an advanced note rather than part of the baseline.

## Workbook C — the Hazard Limit changes, but the draw does not

A five-Dwarf company moves to Moria.

- Moria's normal Hazard draw number is 3.
- The solo procedure therefore draws eight Hazard cards.
- The company's base Hazard Limit is five.
- The Hero player has Hundreds of Butterflies in hand and plays it on a moving character during the Movement/Hazard Phase.

Answer:

1. How many Hazard cards were drawn?
2. What was the company's base Hazard Limit?
3. What is the modified Hazard Limit after Hundreds of Butterflies resolves?
4. Does the Hazard side draw another card?
5. If six legal limit-counting Hazard plays are available, how many can the solo Hazard procedure play?
6. If Twilight is also drawn and has a legal Environment target, does it use one of those six slots?

## Workbook D — the limit is fixed during the phase

A five-character company begins its Movement/Hazard Phase. Its base Hazard Limit is set. During the ensuing attacks, one character is eliminated.

1. Does the base Hazard Limit fall from five to four during this same phase?
2. Would the answer change if the character had left the company before the base limit was set?
3. Why must the Hazard player calculate the limit at the correct step rather than continually recounting the survivors?
