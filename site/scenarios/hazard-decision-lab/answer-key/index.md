# Hazard Decision Lab — Model Answer

[Return to the Hazard Decision Lab]({{ '/scenarios/hazard-decision-lab/' | relative_url }})

The solo instruction is not “play the strongest creature first”. It is to play as many drawn Hazards as possible within the Hazard Limit and choose the sequence that is most disadvantageous for the Hero company. The route, current company, attack wording and remaining support all matter.

## Drill 1 — Moria

### Draw and limit

Moria's normal Hazard draw is 3, so the solo Hazard draw is 2 + (2 × 3) = 8. The six-character company has a base Hazard Limit of 6. The Hero player separately draws at least one and up to two cards from the Hero play deck because the company contains an avatar and characters with mind 3 or greater.

### Legality

| Card | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Despair of the Heart ×3 | **Play all three** | Each may be placed on a different eligible non-Wizard, non-Hobbit character. Dori, Kíli and Óin are three eligible targets. |
| Watcher in the Water | **Play** | Its card explicitly permits play at Moria. |
| Full of Froth and Rage | **Play** | Watcher is an Animal attack, so the +2 prowess effect applies. |
| Wake of War | **Play** | Watcher is an Animal attack, so Wake's +1 prowess effect applies. |
| Seized by Terror | **Do not play** | The declared site path contains Wilderness regions only. Moria's Shadow-hold site type does not satisfy Seized by Terror's Shadow-land/Dark-domain region requirement. |
| Twilight | **Do not play in this example** | There is no Environment card in play or declared earlier in the same chain for Twilight to cancel. Twilight's special timing and no-Hazard-Limit treatment do not remove its target requirement. |

The six legal ordinary plays fill the six slots: three Despairs, Watcher, Full of Froth and Wake. The unplayable Seized by Terror and Twilight are handled under the Academy's supplementary end-of-phase discard convention; Twilight does not count against the limit, but still cannot be played without a legal Environment target.

This is a deliberate stress-test hand, not a typical draw. The chance of seeing at least one of each of Despair, Full of Froth, Wake and Watcher in eight cards is about 7.66%; the exact eight-card composition in the exercise is about 0.00123% (roughly 1 in 81,300). The lesson is how to resolve a legal combination when it occurs, not that this exact line is common.

### The Watcher attack

Watcher in the Water normally gives each character one strike at 8 prowess.

- Full of Froth and Rage adds +2 prowess to the Animal attack.
- Wake of War adds +1 prowess to the Animal attack.
- The resulting Watcher strike prowess is **11**.
- The attack still gives **one strike to each character**.

Wake's extra-strike modifier does not add another strike to Watcher. Council of Elrond Ruling Digest #50 answers this exact question: when an attack specifies that every character faces one strike, an effect that increases the number of strikes by a fixed number has no effect on that attack. Its prowess modifier still applies.

### Why three Despairs are dangerous

Despair of the Heart is a corruption permanent-event. It places 2 corruption points on an eligible target. Each target makes a corruption check every time a character in that target's company becomes wounded. It cannot be duplicated on the same character.

The three Despairs can therefore be placed on three different Dwarves. Dori, Kíli and Óin are particularly punishing targets because each has a −1 corruption-check modification. If a character is wounded by Watcher, each of the three targets makes a corruption check. The Despair ruling says that check occurs before the associated body check.

The Hero player chooses the order in which multiple corruption checks are resolved. Before each roll, other characters in the company may tap to give that check +1 each, but a character tapped to help can contribute to only one of the checks, not all three. This makes the post-Watcher state important: characters already tapped or wounded may leave fewer useful helpers, and the Hero player must allocate the remaining support among three separate checks.

This continues to matter in the Site Phase. If the Hero player enters Moria and a character is wounded by Moria's four Orc strikes at 7, the Despair triggers still apply: the wounded character is in the company of each Despair target. The site automatic attack is separate from the Hazard Limit, but it can still trigger corruption effects already placed on the company.

**Book-bearer decision:** if the Hero player then plays Book of Mazarbûl at Moria, If Gandalf remains untapped and is able to play the item during the Site Phase, he is the safest of these six characters to bear it against this particular Hazard line. Gandalf is a Sage, so he can use the Book's hand-size ability by tapping the Book during the next Organization Phase, and he is a Wizard, so Despair of the Heart cannot be placed on him. If Dori, Kíli or Óin bears the Book while already carrying Despair, that character has 4 corruption points from those two sources (2 from the Book and 2 from Despair). A wound anywhere in the company still triggers the Despair checks for the three Dwarf targets, even if Gandalf bears the Book. This is why the Hero player should consider both the bearer and the company-wide wound trigger, not just the Book's printed 2 corruption points.

If the Hero player declines to enter Moria, the company avoids the automatic attack and cannot play the Book there that turn. The Hazard side should not treat arrival as guaranteed entry.

**Model lesson:** the Moria line attacks the company on two connected levels. Watcher can create wounds during the Movement/Hazard Phase; the Despairs turn those wounds into corruption checks. If the Hero company enters, the separate Moria automatic attack can create further triggers.

## Drill 2 — Goblin-gate

### Draw and limit

Goblin-gate's normal Hazard draw is 2, so the solo Hazard draw is 6. The six-character company's Hazard Limit is also 6. All four declared regions are Wilderness.

### All six drawn cards are legal

| Card | Key / condition | Effect in this hand |
|---|---|---|
| Cave Worm | Named-region key: Redhorn Gate or High Pass appears in the site path | One high-prowess Drake strike; neither support event boosts it. |
| Giant Spiders | Double Wilderness | Spider attack; eligible for both support events. |
| Lesser Spiders | Wilderness | Spider attack; eligible for both support events. |
| Wargs | Wilderness | Wolf attack; eligible for Wake of War, not Full of Froth and Rage. |
| Full of Froth and Rage | Requires a Spider or Animal attack to provide its bonus | +2 prowess to Spider attacks; discard it if a Spider or Animal attack is defeated. |
| Wake of War | Long-event; modifies Wolf, Spider and Animal attacks | +1 prowess and +1 strike to the relevant attacks; no Doors of Night is needed for the normal Wolf/Spider/Animal bonus. |

All six cards can be played, so the six-slot Hazard Limit is filled. The two support events must be in play before the attacks they are intended to modify.

### What each attack becomes

Using the card values in the fixed teaching list:

| Attack | Base | With Wake | With Wake + Full |
|---|---|---|---|
| Giant Spiders | 2 strikes at 10 | 3 strikes at 11 | 3 strikes at **13** |
| Lesser Spiders | 4 strikes at 7 | 5 strikes at 8 | 5 strikes at **10** |
| Wargs | 2 strikes at 9 | 3 strikes at **10** | Full does not apply |
| Cave Worm | 1 strike at 16 | Unchanged | Unchanged |

### The important sequence decision

A creature attack resolves immediately when that creature is played. Full of Froth and Rage is discarded if a Spider or Animal attack is defeated. Therefore, the Hazard player must decide which Spider attack should receive Full's +2 bonus first.

- If the first Spider attack is defeated, Full of Froth and Rage is discarded before the next Spider attack.
- If the first Spider attack is not defeated, Full remains in play and can continue to modify a later Spider/Animal attack.
- Wake of War remains in play for its duration and continues to modify eligible attacks.

There is no universal best Spider order. Giant Spiders has an extra danger beyond its two strikes: if a non-Wizard character wounded by it makes a body check equal to that character's body, that character is discarded. This makes the attack especially threatening to the Dwarf company, and may justify giving Giant Spiders the +2 Full of Froth bonus first. Compare the company's actual body values, prowess, taps, wounds and defensive Resources. A line that makes the Hero spend an important cancellation or defensive card on the first attack may leave the next attack much more dangerous. Do not choose attack order by strike count alone.

A defensible sequence is to establish Wake and Full first, then consider whether Cave Worm's high single strike should force an important defender/resource commitment before the Spider attacks. After that, choose which Spider attack should receive Full's bonus first. This is a tactical judgement, not a compulsory order.

### Goblin-gate's automatic attack

Goblin-gate has a separate automatic attack of three Orc strikes at 6 prowess. It is faced only if the Hero company enters during the Site Phase. It is not one of the six drawn Hazards and does not use the six Hazard Limit slots. Neither Full of Froth and Rage nor Wake of War modifies this Orc attack.

## Drill 3 — Blue Mountain Dwarf-hold

### Draw and limit

Blue Mountain's normal Hazard draw is 1, so the solo Hazard draw is 4. The six-character company's base Hazard Limit remains 6, but only four cards were drawn.

### Legal cards

| Card | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Cave Worm | **Play** | High Pass is a named region in the site path. |
| Giant Spiders | **Play** | The route contains four Wilderness regions, satisfying double Wilderness. |
| Wargs | **Play** | Wilderness is a legal key. |
| Seized by Terror | **Do not play** | The route contains no Shadow-land or Dark-domain. A Dwarf-hold site name/type does not replace the required region type. |

Three of the four cards are legal. Play those three; the Hazard Limit of six is a ceiling, not permission to invent additional cards or draw more than the solo procedure provides. Blue Mountain Dwarf-hold has no automatic attack in this Hero Location deck.

## Route comparison — what changed?

| Route leg | Normal Hazard draw | Solo draw | Main lesson |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Rivendell → Moria | 3 | 8 | Watcher is legal at Moria; three Despairs can target different Dwarves. |
| Moria → Goblin-gate | 2 | 6 | Four Wilderness regions make both Spider and Warg keys available; Cave Worm has named-region keys. |
| Goblin-gate → Blue Mountain | 1 | 4 | The same Wilderness creature keys remain, but the smaller draw means fewer cards to select. |

The company size has not changed, so the base Hazard Limit stays at six across the three examples. The number of cards drawn changes with the destination's normal Hazard draw number. These are separate calculations.

## What to take into a real game

1. **Legality first:** a powerful card with no legal key or target is not a play.
2. **Count the draw and the limit separately:** draw volume is not play capacity.
3. **Sequence support before attacks:** an attack resolves as soon as its creature is played.
4. **Watch conditional support:** Full of Froth and Rage can disappear after a defeated Spider/Animal attack.
5. **Remember the Site Phase:** Despair triggers can continue when the company later faces an automatic attack.
6. **Do not force a fixed ranking:** the best line depends on the actual Hero company and the cards drawn.
