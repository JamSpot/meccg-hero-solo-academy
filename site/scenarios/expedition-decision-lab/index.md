# Expedition Decision Lab

This lab joins the Academy's core skills into one repeatable exercise: build a plan with the Hero deck, declare legal Region Movement, forecast the Hazard deck, decide whether to attempt the objective, and choose a sensible next expedition.

Use the genuine physical **Dwarven Quest / Pallando Challenge Deck C** teaching lists. No proxies or extra cards are required. The situations below are practice states; where a decision depends on a card's wording, read the physical card rather than guessing from its name.

## How to run the lab

For each case, write down:

1. the mission;
2. the legal route and its region count;
3. the Hazard exposure created by that route;
4. the resources you need now versus those you may need later;
5. the decision you will make and the rule/card text supporting it;
6. what new information would change your decision.

Do not read the model answers until you have made a decision. A different answer can be reasonable if it is legal and supported by the actual game state.

## Case 1 — The first expedition

**State:** The five-character starting company is at Rivendell. The mission is to reach Moria and acquire Book of Mazarbul. You have the standard Game 1 Hero and Hazard teaching decks.

**Route proposal:** Rhudaur → Hollin → Redhorn Gate.

**Your tasks**

- Count the regions, including the origin and destination.
- Check each adjacency on your physical region cards.
- Identify the region types exposed by the route.
- Explain why the site path is relevant to the Hazard player but does not itself move the company.
- State the solo Hazard draw and base Hazard Limit separately under the Academy's chosen procedure.
- Name two things you must reassess before attempting the objective after arrival.

**Record:** Is this a legal route? What is its main strategic cost?

## Case 2 — The hand is not the plan

**State:** You are preparing the same expedition. Your hand contains a mix of movement, avoidance, combat, and acquisition resources. You are tempted to keep every card because it might be useful later.

**Your tasks**

- Sort the cards into **needed now**, **needed later**, and **low value for this plan**.
- Explain why a card that is not playable this turn is not automatically a bad card.
- Explain why keeping every card forever can also be a mistake.
- Choose one card you would be reluctant to discard and state what future situation would justify keeping it. Check its printed text before deciding.

**Record:** Which card are you keeping for a specific plan rather than out of habit?

## Case 3 — Eight drawn, five is the limit

**State:** The five-character company moves to Moria. Under the Academy's selected solo procedure, eight Hazard cards are drawn. Five or more cards look potentially useful, but each must be checked for legality and timing.

**Your tasks**

- Explain what the draw of eight means and what the base Hazard Limit of five means.
- Check each candidate card against the declared site path, destination, target, timing and printed conditions.
- Choose the strongest legal combination under the selected solo procedure.
- Explain why an invalid creature key cannot be rescued by an unused Hazard Limit slot.
- Record any card interaction you cannot verify from the printed text and official rulings.

**Record:** Which candidate did you reject, and why?

## Case 4 — Arrive, then decide

**State:** The company reaches Moria, but its best fighter is wounded, two characters are tapped, and the most useful avoidance resource has already been spent.

**Your tasks**

- Check Moria's site requirements and automatic attack using the physical site card and rules.
- Assess the company's actual ability to handle the attack and any remaining legal support.
- Compare attempting the objective now with delaying or choosing a recovery plan.
- State what would make you change your mind.

**Record:** Do not answer “always enter” or “always retreat.” Write the facts that make your choice sensible.

## Case 5 — The next expedition is not automatic

**State:** The company has completed its Moria objective and is considering Blue Mountain Dwarf-hold as a next destination.

**Proposed direct route:** Redhorn Gate → Hollin → Rhudaur → Arthedain → Númeriador.

**Your tasks**

- Count the regions, including both endpoints.
- Explain whether this is legal as one normal Region Movement.
- Split the journey into legal legs using the verified teaching example, or propose another route only after checking its adjacency and site availability.
- Compare the cost of an intermediate stop with the benefit of recovery, hand management, and future positioning.
- Decide whether the company's condition justifies continuing the campaign immediately.

**Record:** What does the extra stop buy you, beyond merely satisfying the movement rule?

## Case 6 — Same destination, different exposure

**State:** You identify two legal routes to the same objective. Route A exposes a region type that makes a creature in the drawn Hazard hand legal. Route B has a different exposure but leaves the company better positioned for the following expedition.

**Your tasks**

- Check both routes' legality and region count.
- Identify the actual creature's printed key before deciding whether it is legal on Route A.
- Compare objective value, likely Hazard pressure, company condition, and future position.
- Explain why the best route for the Hero player may differ from the best route for the Hazard player.
- Choose a route and state what evidence would make you reconsider.

**Record:** Which trade-off mattered most: immediate safety, objective value, or next-turn position?

After the third game, classify each notable card as:

- **Not seen** — no judgement yet.
- **Seen but illegal** — check whether the route made it unusable.
- **Legal but low impact** — describe the actual board state.
- **Useful in a confirmed situation** — cite the card text/ruling.
- **Needs more testing** — keep the list unchanged and test again.

## Completion standard

You are ready to move beyond this lab when you can consistently:

- declare a legal route without counting errors;
- distinguish movement route from site path;
- explain Hazard draw versus Hazard Limit;
- identify a legal threat without relying on card names or assumptions;
- justify a resource decision;
- assess the company before entering a site;
- plan the next leg without assuming every expedition must return to a Haven;
- and describe what evidence would change your decision.
