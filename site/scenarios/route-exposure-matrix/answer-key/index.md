# Route Exposure Matrix — Model Answer

The main finding is that the three teaching routes have different Hazard draws and site attacks, but **the same broad region-type exposure**: every region in every declared route is Wilderness. Most of the baseline creature package is therefore legal on all three routes.

## 1. Route calculations

| Journey | Normal Hazard draw | Solo Hazard draw | Hero draw | Five-Dwarf base Hazard Limit | Site attack if entered |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Rivendell → Moria | 3 | **8** | Up to 2 | **5** | Orcs — 4 strikes at 7 |
| Moria → Goblin-gate | 2 | **6** | Up to 1 | **5** | Orcs — 3 strikes at 6 |
| Goblin-gate → Blue Mountain Dwarf-hold | 1 | **4** | Up to 1 | **5** | None |

The formula is applied to the normal Hazard draw number on the destination site card for these non-Haven moves: 2 + twice that number. The Hero draw is a separate draw from the Hero play deck. The Hazard Limit is a ceiling on ordinary Hazard plays, not a second draw formula and not the number of automatic-attack strikes.

For a move to a Haven, use the origin site's draw numbers. For example, Moria → Rivendell uses Moria's 3/2 values: eight solo Hazards and up to two Hero cards, not Rivendell's own values.

## 3. Why the routes do not change the creature package very much

All three route paths are entirely Wilderness:

- **Rivendell → Moria:** Rhudaur, Hollin, Redhorn Gate.
- **Moria → Goblin-gate:** Redhorn Gate, Hollin, Rhudaur, High Pass.
- **Goblin-gate → Blue Mountain:** High Pass, Rhudaur, Arthedain, Númeriador.

The named-region key changes for Cave Worm, but every route contains at least one of its listed regions. The double-Wilderness and single-Wilderness creature keys are satisfied throughout. The important pressure changes are the number of Hazard cards drawn, the site's automatic attack, the objective and the company's arrival condition.

This is why the current list has a coherent teaching identity: it can challenge the company across the connected expedition without relying on Dragons or changing the deck between legs. It still has two acknowledged conditional draws—Seized by Terror and Twilight—on this all-Wilderness, no-Environment campaign.

## 4. Constructed eight-card hand

The exercise hand contains six usable or conditionally usable cards:

| Card | Answer | Hazard Limit use |
|---|---|---:|
| Cave Worm | Legal through a named-region key | 1 |
| Giant Spiders | Legal through double Wilderness | 1 |
| Lesser Spiders | Legal through Wilderness | 1 |
| Full of Froth and Rage | Play when an eligible Spider/Animal attack is present | 1 |
| Wake of War | Play when an eligible Wolf/Spider/Animal attack is present | 1 |
| River | Legal on the destination site while the company is moving there | 1 |
| Seized by Terror | Illegal: no Shadow-land or Dark-domain | 0 |
| Twilight | No legal Environment target in the baseline | 0 |

Six cards are available to consider, but only five ordinary Hazard plays fit the five-character company's base Hazard Limit. Twilight would not use a slot if it had a legal target; in this state it has no target and is not a useful play.

### One defensible attack-heavy line

A possible line is:

1. Play one Spider creature.
2. In the same chain, play Full of Froth and Rage and Wake of War before the attack resolves.
3. Resolve the attack with both modifiers.
4. If the Spider/Animal attack is defeated, discard Full of Froth and Rage immediately. Its +2 prowess will not help a later Spider/Animal attack.
5. Play the other Spider creature and Cave Worm if they remain the most damaging legal options.

This line uses four or five Hazard Limit slots depending on whether River is chosen instead of one creature. The exact selection depends on the company's remaining fighters, taps, wounds, equipment and the value of the Site Phase objective.

### One defensible disruption line

Replace one creature attack with River if denying the Site Phase is more valuable than the extra attack. The Hero player can tap Óin to cancel River, but that spends a Ranger's action. If Óin is already tapped or needed for another purpose, River can deny the company's site actions for the turn.

Do not call either line universally optimal. The solo instruction is to choose the most disadvantageous legal combination **for the actual company and mission**.

**Strike-count check:** Wake of War's +1 strike does not add extra strikes to attacks that specify that each character faces one strike, such as Neeker-breekers and Watcher in the Water. Their applicable prowess modifiers still apply. By contrast, Giant Spiders and Lesser Spiders can receive both Full of Froth and Rage (+2 prowess) and Wake of War (+1 prowess and +1 strike). If a Spider or Animal attack is defeated, Full of Froth and Rage is discarded before a later attack; do not keep applying its +2 bonus.

## 5. The two proposed replacements

- **Arouse Minions:** a legal candidate at Moria and Goblin-gate because both are Shadow-holds. It cannot target an automatic attack at Blue Mountain because that site is a Free-hold and has no automatic attack. Its +3 prowess matters only if the company enters and faces the modified attack. It is not in the baseline list.
- **Brigands:** a legal candidate on all three routes through the Wilderness key. It has two strikes at 8 prowess; if any strike wounds a character, the company discards one item of the defender's choice. It is not in the baseline list.

Neither candidate justifies changing the fixed list before the first three baseline games.

## 6. Fair-play debrief

After each route, record:

- the actual eight-, six- or four-card Hazard draw;
- every card that was legal, conditional or illegal;
- the reason for every unused card;
- which five or fewer plays were chosen and why;
- whether Full of Froth and Rage survived;
- whether River forced Óin to tap or denied the Site Phase;
- whether the company entered and faced the automatic attack;
- the condition of the company at the end of the turn.

This record lets you assess whether the Hazard deck is genuinely challenging without making illegal plays or inventing extra attacks.
