# Hazard Deck Balance Lab — Model Answer

Use this after attempting the [Hazard Deck Balance Lab]({{ '/scenarios/hazard-deck-balance-lab/' | relative_url }}). The purpose is to make every decision traceable to a card, site, route or rule.

## 1. Why the four baseline cards are campaign-inactive

**Seized by Terror ×2** requires a non-Wizard, non-Ringwraith character moving in a Shadow-land or Dark-domain region. The three Academy site paths are Wilderness-only. Moria and Goblin-gate have the **Shadow-hold site type**, but that is not the same thing as a **Shadow-land region type**. The printed key is not met.

**Twilight ×2** cancels an Environment card in play or declared earlier in the same chain. The fixed Hero main deck has no Environment Resource, the sideboard is closed, and the Hazard list has no other Environment. Twilight is not a generic cancellation card and cannot be played merely to discard it.

These cards are conditional, not universally useless. A later campaign that includes Shadow-land/Dark-domain movement or an Environment can make them relevant.

## 2. Why Arouse Minions changes by destination

Arouse Minions is playable on a Shadow-hold or Dark-hold and increases the prowess of one automatic attack at that site by three for the turn.

- **Moria:** Hero site type Shadow-hold; its four Orc strikes rise from 7 to 10 prowess.
- **Goblin-gate:** Hero site type Shadow-hold; its three Orc strikes rise from 6 to 9 prowess.
- **Blue Mountain Dwarf-hold:** Hero site type Free-hold; Arouse Minions is not playable there.

It does not add strikes. It is a Hazard short-event, so it uses one ordinary Hazard Limit slot. The automatic attack itself is not a Hazard play.

The automatic attack can be affected while the site is in play during the Movement/Hazard Phase. The Hero player still decides whether to enter the site; the Hazard player must not pretend that the company is forced to face the attack merely because Arouse Minions was played.

## 3. What Brigands actually threatens

Brigands is a Men creature with two strikes at 8 prowess, keyed to Border-land or Wilderness. All three declared route paths include Wilderness, so it has a legal route key on each.

If any Brigands strike wounds a character, the company must immediately discard **one** item, chosen by the defender. It is not one item per wound, and the Hazard player does not choose which item is lost.

If no character is wounded, the item-discard clause does not happen. The creature can still threaten wounds and taps, but its special item pressure has not been realised. If the company has no item in play, the special clause has no item to remove.

## 4. The baseline Moria hand

Assume the fixed eight-card hand is:

- Cave Worm
- Giant Spiders
- Lesser Spiders
- Full of Froth and Rage
- Wake of War
- River
- Seized by Terror
- Twilight

The five-Dwarf company is moving Rivendell → Moria through Rhudaur, Hollin and Redhorn Gate. Óin is an untapped Ranger. No Environment is in play.

| Card | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Cave Worm | Legal | Redhorn Gate is a named-region key; one strike at 16 prowess. |
| Giant Spiders | Legal | The path contains at least two Wilderness regions; two strikes at 10 before modifiers. |
| Lesser Spiders | Legal | The Wilderness region key is present; four strikes at 7 before modifiers. |
| Full of Froth and Rage | Legal | A Spider attack is available; +2 prowess to Spider and Animal attacks. |
| Wake of War | Legal | Wolf, Spider and Animal attacks are available; +1 strike and +1 prowess to each such attack. |
| River | Legal | The company is moving to Moria. Óin may tap to cancel it; that costs the company an action. |
| Seized by Terror | Illegal | No Shadow-land or Dark-domain region in the site path. |
| Twilight | No legal target | No Environment card is in play or earlier in the chain. |

There are six legal candidates and a five-slot Hazard Limit. The Hazard player must compare the impact of the legal options rather than automatically selecting the first five.

### A combat-pressure line

One defensible five-slot line is:

1. Wake of War.
2. Full of Froth and Rage.
3. Giant Spiders.
4. Lesser Spiders.
5. Cave Worm.

Wake of War makes the Spider attacks +1 strike and +1 prowess. Full of Froth and Rage adds a further +2 prowess while it remains in play. Choose which Spider attack to lead with based on the actual company: compare its available prowess, taps, strike assignments and defensive resources. There is no universal order. If the first Spider attack is defeated, Full of Froth and Rage is discarded before the next Spider attack; if it survives, the bonus remains for the next one.

Cave Worm is unaffected by either support event. It is a separate one-strike, 16-prowess threat.

### A disruption line

A different company state may justify:

1. Wake of War.
2. Full of Froth and Rage.
3. Giant Spiders.
4. Lesser Spiders.
5. River instead of Cave Worm.

River can force Óin to tap to preserve the Site Phase. If Óin is needed for a later action or corruption-check support, this may be more damaging than adding the Cave Worm strike. If the company can spare Óin, or has no important Site Phase action, Cave Worm may be the stronger fifth play.

There is no universal answer without the actual board state. The solitaire Hazard side should choose the line most disadvantageous to the Hero company **in that state**, while respecting all printed restrictions.

## 5. The variant Moria hand

Assume a later test hand contains:

- Cave Worm
- Giant Spiders
- Lesser Spiders
- Full of Froth and Rage
- Wake of War
- River
- Arouse Minions
- Brigands

All eight have a potential legal play on the Moria route. That does not mean all eight should be played:

- Arouse Minions can raise Moria's automatic attack to four Orc strikes at 10 prowess, but costs one of the five Hazard slots.
- Brigands can be keyed to Wilderness. It is more valuable if the company is carrying an item that it cannot afford to lose.
- River competes with the attack cards for a slot, but can force Óin to tap.
- The two support events also use slots; they are not free additions to the attacks.

A possible pressure line is Arouse Minions, Wake of War, Full of Froth and Rage, Giant Spiders and Lesser Spiders. This attacks the site's automatic attack and the company's ability to survive the Spider attacks, but it gives up River, Cave Worm and Brigands. If the company has an important item, Brigands may be more valuable; if Óin's tap matters, River may be more valuable.

At Blue Mountain Dwarf-hold, Arouse Minions is illegal because the destination is a Free-hold. Brigands remains keyed by the Wilderness route.

## 6. Blue Mountain variant check

On Goblin-gate → Blue Mountain Dwarf-hold, the destination is a Free-hold. Arouse Minions is not legal on that destination, even though the company is leaving a Shadow-hold: during this movement/hazard phase the event must target the company's new site, and the new site does not meet the card's Shadow-hold/Dark-hold requirement. Brigands remains legal because the declared site path contains Wilderness. The normal Hazard draw is one, so the solo draw is 2 + (2 × 1) = **4 cards**. The five-Dwarf Hazard Limit is still five, but only four cards have been drawn, so no more than four drawn cards can be played.

## 7. Why the probability figures are not win probabilities

The table uses a hypergeometric draw from a fresh 30-card deck. For example, the baseline Moria draw takes eight cards from a list containing 16 creatures and 14 non-creatures. The expected number of creatures is 8 × 16/30 = 4.27.

The chance of drawing at least one of the four campaign-inactive cards is 1 − [C(26,8) / C(30,8)] ≈ 73.3%.

That does not mean 73.3% of Moria turns are weak. The other cards may be conditional, a drawn creature may be strategically inferior to an event, and the Hazard Limit is five. The probability is only a way to compare list composition before play.

The proposed variant has 17 creatures, so the expected Moria creature count rises to \(8 \times 17/30 = 4.53\). Its chance of drawing at least one campaign-inactive card falls to 46.9% on the Moria route, but this says nothing by itself about whether the Hero company is more likely to be wounded or lose the Book.

## 8. The actual decision rule

Keep the baseline until three games have been recorded. Test the two-card variant only if the baseline shows a repeated problem and you already own the cards.

Change no other deck or rule at the same time. Compare the number of legal plays, actual Hazard Limit use, wounds, taps, corruption checks, item losses, denied Site Phase actions and objective progress.

A variant that produces more legal options but no greater consequence may improve variety without improving difficulty. That is useful evidence, and it is a reason not to add more cards blindly.
