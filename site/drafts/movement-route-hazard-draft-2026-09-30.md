# Hero Solo Academy — Movement, Route, Hazard & Practice Draft

## Research baseline — 4 October 2026

This chapter separates three things that are often confused:

**Movement route ≠ site path ≠ Hazard exposure.**

The Academy uses the historical Pallando Dwarven Quest Challenge Deck as a benchmark, but does not require copying its full 110-card list. The historical player aid identifies Thorin II controlling Dori and Kili, Gloin controlling Oin, and the core acceleration package of Bounty of the Hoard, Lucky Search and Thror's Map. It also describes Fellowship for corruption protection, early avoidance, and a creature-swarm Hazard strategy.

## 1. The six-gate movement decision

Before moving a company, ask:

1. **LEGALITY** — Is the proposed movement legal?
2. **DESTINATION** — What does the destination accomplish?
3. **MOVEMENT TYPE** — Which movement method is appropriate?
4. **SITE PATH** — What path is being declared for Hazard purposes?
5. **EXPOSURE** — What does that path allow the Hazard player to key?
6. **POSITION** — Where will the company be when the expedition ends?

### Captain's rule

> The shortest legal journey is not automatically the best journey.

## 2. “Free movement” in the Academy

The Academy uses **free movement** as a teaching phrase for movement where the Hero player chooses the destination and, when using Region Movement, the legal region path rather than simply following the nearest-haven Starter Movement network.

It does **not** mean unlimited movement.

The normal Region Movement rule is still a maximum of four consecutive regions, including both the origin and destination, with no repeated region. The current Council rules explicitly define this limit.

So:

**A → B → C → D = four regions.**

**A → B → C → D → E = five regions and is not a normal legal Region Movement path.**

This is the first movement skill to master: **freedom of destination is constrained by movement legality.**

## 3. Starter Movement versus Region Movement

This distinction is central to the Academy.

### Starter Movement

Starter Movement is the structured route between sites based on the nearest-haven relationships printed on the site cards. The current rules allow it when the appropriate haven relationship is listed, or when both sites are havens with a listed site path to one another.

For beginners, Starter Movement has an important advantage: its company site path normally identifies only the origin and destination region names, rather than naming every intervening region. The Council explicitly describes this as typically safer for beginners.

### Region Movement

Region Movement gives the Hero player much greater route freedom. The company can travel through any legal sequence of adjacent regions within the four-region limit.

But that freedom creates a cost:

> **Every named region in the Region Movement path is information available to the Hazard player.**

The current rules state that the Region Movement site path contains the types **and names of all regions being moved through**.

### Academy rule of thumb

**Use Starter Movement when its safer exposure is valuable.**

**Use Region Movement when destination, route control or future position makes the extra exposure worthwhile.**

Do not teach “Region Movement is better.” Teach:

> **Region Movement gives you more control and gives the Hazard player more information.**

## 4. Region Movement: the four-region drill

A repeated region is not a valid way to stretch the route.

The Academy drill is physical:

1. Put the origin region down.
2. Add each intervening region.
3. Add the destination region.
4. Count the cards.
5. Check adjacency.
6. Check that no region repeats.

Do this before considering the Hazard cards.

## 5. Maps are aids, not authority

Current official rules make region-card text authoritative when it differs from a map.

**Captain's habit:** when a route matters, verify the region cards rather than trusting the picture.

## 6. The declared site path is not the movement itself

This is an important advanced rule.

The site path is used to determine what Hazards may key to the company. It does not physically move the company across the map.

For Region Movement, the declared site path can be different from the site's printed site path, provided the declared route was legal when movement was declared.

This produces a useful Academy distinction:

**Route choice determines where the company can go.**

**Site-path declaration determines much of what the Hazard player can legally attack it with.**

The two concepts interact, but they are not identical.

## 7. Hazard exposure

Creature Hazards can key to matching region types/names in the company's site path or to the new site's type/name. Multiple matching region symbols can require multiple occurrences of that region type.

Therefore, route planning must include a Hazard forecast:

**What did I expose?**

**What can now legally be played?**

**What attack is most dangerous?**

**What can I afford to ignore?**

## 8. The Hazard player

The Hazard Limit is a ceiling, not a requirement to spend every available card.

The base Hazard Limit is set when the company's Movement/Hazard Phase begins: company size or two, whichever is greater, rounded up. If the Hazard player accessed the sideboard during that turn's untap phase, the base limit is halved, rounded up. The limit then remains fixed for that phase even if the company changes size.

The Academy Hazard procedure is:

**LEGALITY → CRITICAL CHARACTER → DISRUPTION → SEQUENCING → PRESSURE BUDGET**

Ask:

- Who is easiest to disrupt?
- Who is most important to the expedition?
- Who is already vulnerable?
- Who is needed immediately afterwards?
- What changes if this character is wounded, tapped or removed?
- Is this Hazard better now or later?

## 9. Hero deck teaching arc

The Apprentice Hero Resource deck is taught in seven functions:

**MOVE → AVOID → SURVIVE → FIGHT → ACQUIRE → COMPRESS → DEVELOP**

The important question is:

> **What expedition problem does this card solve, and what future value am I spending to solve it now?**

### Core teaching package

The first physical deck should prioritise:

- **Book of Mazarbul** — objective transformation.
- **Fellowship** — corruption management and future protection.
- **Concealment / Not at Home** — avoidance and preservation of future turns.
- **Marvels Told** — hazard interaction and timing.
- **Great-road** — movement efficiency.
- **Bounty of the Hoard** — expedition/resource compression.
- **Lucky Search** — site-specific opportunity and risk/reward.
- **Thror's Map** — stored value and route/resource planning.
- **A Friend or Three** — company development.
- **Smoke Rings** — flexibility and future options.
- **Risky Blow** — combat decision-making.

The historical player guide specifically describes Bounty of the Hoard, Lucky Search and Thror's Map as the Dwarven deck's acceleration package and warns that Thror's Map should not be casually discarded.

## 10. Complementary Hazard deck

The first Hazard deck should teach rather than simply maximise kills.

### Creature teaching core

Use a manageable creature package based on the historical Dwarven Quest philosophy:

- Cave Worm
- Giant Spiders
- Lesser Spiders
- Wargs
- Watcher in the Water

### Amplification

- Full of Froth and Rage
- Wake of War

### Non-creature pressure

- River
- Despair of the Heart
- Seized by Terror
- Twilight

The historical deck's Hazard strategy was explicitly based around Animals and Wolves, with Full of Froth and Rage and Wake of War as amplifiers.

For the Academy, however, the Hazard deck is deliberately treated as a **teaching opponent**, not as an attempt to recreate the most oppressive possible tournament Hazard deck.

## 11. Route example: Rivendell → Moria

**Objective:** acquire Book of Mazarbul.

Evaluate:

**Legality → movement type → exposure → arrival condition → combat risk → recovery → next objective**

The first question is not “Can I get to Moria?”

It is:

> **Which legal movement method gets me there while preserving the most campaign value?**

After acquiring the Book, stop and recalculate.

The expedition changes from:

**ACQUIRE**

to:

**SECURE → CONSOLIDATE → REPOSITION**

## 12. Route example: same destination, different exposure

When two legal routes reach the same site, compare:

| Factor | Route A | Route B |
|---|---|---|
| Legality | ✓ | ✓ |
| Length | Equal | Equal |
| Movement type | Starter/Region | Region |
| Named region exposure | Lower | Higher |
| Creature exposure | Lower | Higher |
| Recovery | Better | Worse |
| Next objective | Strong | Weak |
| Immediate danger | Lower | Higher |

Do not memorise “always take the safe route.”

> **Choose the route whose total expedition value is highest.**

## 13. Practice scenarios

### Scenario 1 — Four Means Four
Five region cards are proposed.

**Lesson:** count cards, including origin and destination.

### Scenario 2 — Starter or Region?
A destination is reachable by Starter Movement, but Region Movement would give a more useful follow-up position.

**Lesson:** compare control gained against additional Hazard exposure.

### Scenario 3 — The Map Trap
The map suggests adjacency but region-card text differs.

**Lesson:** use the authoritative region rule.

### Scenario 4 — Declared Path
The player assumes the printed site path is the only possible Region Movement path.

**Lesson:** the declared Region Movement path can differ from a site's printed path, provided the movement was legal.

### Scenario 5 — Stored Value
Thror's Map is playable but has little immediate value.

**Lesson:** a card can be valuable while remaining unplayed.

### Scenario 6 — Avoidance
A strong creature attacks a vulnerable company.

**Lesson:** compare avoidance, combat and post-combat state.

### Scenario 7 — Hazard Budget
Five Hazard cards are legal.

**Lesson:** five legal cards do not imply five good plays.

### Scenario 8 — Critical Character
The strongest fighter is not required for the next objective.

**Lesson:** objective importance can beat raw prowess.

### Scenario 9 — Objective Transformation
Book of Mazarbul is acquired.

**Lesson:** re-plan immediately.

### Scenario 10 — Recovery
The company can press on or recover.

**Lesson:** compare immediate gain with future-turn risk.

### Scenario 11 — Compression
Two MP-producing opportunities can be combined.

**Lesson:** look for plays that make one expedition accomplish multiple jobs.

### Scenario 12 — Abort
The company has wounds, tapped characters and poor cards.

**Lesson:** controlled retreat can preserve campaign value.

### Scenario 13 — The Exposed Wilderness
Region Movement creates an additional Wilderness occurrence in the declared path.

**Question:** What new creature-keying possibilities did you give the Hazard player?

**Lesson:** route freedom creates information cost.

### Scenario 14 — The Safer Haven Route
Starter Movement is available, but Region Movement would shorten the strategic route.

**Question:** Is the extra exposure worth the positional gain?

**Lesson:** movement type is itself a strategic choice.

### Scenario 15 — The Fixed Limit
A character is eliminated during the Movement/Hazard Phase.

**Question:** Does the company's Hazard Limit immediately shrink?

**Lesson:** No. The base limit was fixed at the beginning of the phase.

## 14. Physical-card acquisition tiers

### Tier 1 — Play immediately
Prioritise the Dwarven starting company, the chosen Hero/Wizard teaching setup, Book of Mazarbul, core movement/avoidance resources, a small creature Hazard package, and the sites/regions needed for the first route.

### Tier 2 — Strongly recommended
Add Bounty of the Hoard, Lucky Search, Thror's Map, Fellowship, Marvels Told, Great-road, Not at Home, A Friend or Three, Smoke Rings and combat support.

### Tier 3 — Campaign expansion
Add more Dwarves, item options, allies/factions and alternate routes.

### Tier 4 — Advanced
Introduce Dragons and more sophisticated Hazard interactions after the first expedition is reliable.

## Academy stopping rule

Do not delay Game 1 while collecting the complete historical Challenge Deck.

The target is a **minimum playable teaching pool** first. Once that pool is physically assembled, play begins and the deck evolves through actual games.
